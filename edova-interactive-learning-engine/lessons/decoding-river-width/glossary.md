# Glossary — Decoding River Width

## Angle of Sight

- **Definition:** The angle measured between two lines of sight — typically
  between a baseline and the direction to a distant target — used as the
  known angle in a triangle for indirect measurement.
- **Example:** Standing at point B, the angle between the baseline (toward
  A) and the line of sight to the tree at C is the angle of sight, measured
  as 30° in the lesson's worked example.
- **Usage:** "Measure the angle of sight from point B to the tree using the
  clinometer."

## Angle of Elevation

- **Definition:** The angle between a horizontal line and the line of sight
  to an object that is above the horizontal (looking upward).
- **Example:** Looking up at the top of a flagpole from the ground, the
  angle between your horizontal line of sight and your line of sight to the
  top of the pole is the angle of elevation.
- **Usage:** "The angle of elevation to the top of the tower was measured
  as 42°."

## Line of Sight

- **Definition:** The straight, unobstructed line from an observer's eye
  (or an instrument) to the object being observed.
- **Example:** The line from the surveyor at point A to the base of the
  tree at point C is the line of sight used to define the river's width.
- **Usage:** "There must be a clear line of sight to the target for the
  angle measurement to be valid."

## Baseline

- **Definition:** A known, measurable distance walked and marked on
  accessible ground, used as the "known side" of the triangle in an
  indirect measurement.
- **Example:** Walking 40 m along the near riverbank from point A to point
  B creates the baseline AB used in the calculation.
- **Usage:** "A longer baseline generally produces a more reliable angle
  measurement, up to a practical limit."

## Tangent Ratio

- **Definition:** In a right triangle, the ratio of the length of the side
  opposite a given angle to the length of the side adjacent to it:
  `tan(θ) = opposite / adjacent`.
- **Example:** In triangle ABC, `tan(B) = AC / AB`, which is rearranged to
  find the unknown width `AC = AB × tan(B)`.
- **Usage:** "Because we know one side and one angle, and want the side
  opposite that angle, the tangent ratio is the correct tool."

## Opposite Side

- **Definition:** In a right triangle, the side that does not touch the
  angle being considered.
- **Example:** Relative to angle B, side AC (the river width) is the
  opposite side.
- **Usage:** "The opposite side is on the far side of the triangle from the
  angle you're using."

## Adjacent Side

- **Definition:** In a right triangle, the side that touches the angle
  being considered but is not the hypotenuse.
- **Example:** Relative to angle B, side AB (the baseline) is the adjacent
  side.
- **Usage:** "The baseline is adjacent to angle B, since it forms one arm
  of that angle."

## Hypotenuse

- **Definition:** The longest side of a right triangle, always opposite the
  90° angle.
- **Example:** In triangle ABC, side BC (from the baseline point to the
  tree) is the hypotenuse, though it is never directly used in this
  lesson's calculation.
- **Usage:** "The hypotenuse is not needed here because the tangent ratio
  only involves the opposite and adjacent sides."

## Clinometer

- **Definition:** A handheld instrument used to measure angles of
  elevation, depression, or sight relative to a horizontal or reference
  line.
- **Example:** The surveyor uses a clinometer to measure the 30° angle of
  sight from point B to the tree at C.
- **Usage:** "Modern smartphone apps can approximate a clinometer using the
  device's built-in accelerometer."

## Theodolite

- **Definition:** A precision optical instrument for measuring both
  horizontal and vertical angles, used extensively in professional
  surveying.
- **Example:** A theodolite would be used instead of a simple clinometer
  for a professional land survey requiring higher accuracy than the
  classroom example needs.
- **Usage:** "Large-scale surveys typically use a theodolite rather than a
  handheld clinometer for greater precision."

## Similar Triangles

- **Definition:** Two triangles whose corresponding angles are equal and
  whose corresponding sides are in the same proportion, even if their sizes
  differ.
- **Example:** The verification triangle formed with the second baseline
  point D shares angle A and the side AC with the original triangle, making
  the two triangles related through the same underlying geometry.
- **Usage:** "Similar triangles are the alternative method for solving this
  problem without trigonometric ratios."

## Indirect Measurement

- **Definition:** The technique of determining a distance or dimension that
  cannot be measured directly, by instead measuring related, accessible
  quantities (such as a baseline and an angle) and calculating the desired
  value.
- **Example:** Finding the width of a river without crossing it is a
  classic example of indirect measurement.
- **Usage:** "Indirect measurement is essential whenever the distance in
  question is physically inaccessible or unsafe to measure directly."
