# Lesson Outline — Decoding River Width

## Lesson Overview

"Decoding River Width" teaches students how to find the width of a river —
a distance that cannot be measured directly — using the tangent ratio from
trigonometry. Students follow a field surveyor's problem from start to
finish: mark a baseline on dry land, measure one angle with an instrument,
apply `tan(angle) = opposite / adjacent`, solve for the unknown side, and
then verify the answer with a second, independent measurement. The lesson
models the full arc of applied mathematics — problem framing, setup,
calculation, and verification — rather than presenting trigonometry as an
isolated formula to memorize.

This lesson is the **reference / gold-standard lesson** for the platform.
Its structure, pacing, and depth of interaction should be used as the
template for every future AI-generated lesson.

## Prerequisites

Students should already be able to:

- Identify the sides of a right triangle (opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse)
  relative to a given angle.
- State and recall the three basic trigonometric ratios: sine, cosine,
  tangent.
- Evaluate `tan(30°)`, `tan(45°)`, `tan(60°)` from the standard trigonometric
  ratio table.
- Perform basic algebraic rearrangement of an equation to isolate an unknown
  (e.g., solving `a = b × c` for `c`).
- Read and interpret a simple labelled diagram.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1. Explain why some real-world distances must be measured indirectly.
2. Define "angle of elevation" and "line of sight" in a measurement context.
3. Construct a right triangle model from a real-world scenario, correctly
   identifying the baseline, the right angle, and the unknown side.
4. Apply the tangent ratio to calculate an unknown distance from one known
   side and one known angle.
5. Evaluate the reliability of a measurement by cross-checking it with a
   second, independent triangle.
6. Design a measurement plan (baseline placement and angle to measure) for a
   new, unseen indirect-measurement scenario.

## Concept Flow

The lesson moves through six concepts in a single, unbroken problem-solving
arc (no concept is revisited out of order):

1. **The Indirect Measurement Problem** — why we cannot simply walk across
   the river with a tape measure.
2. **Angle of Elevation and Line of Sight** — the vocabulary and instrument
   behind the one measurable angle.
3. **Constructing the Right Triangle** — turning the riverbank and a tree
   into a labelled triangle with a baseline and a right angle.
4. **The Tangent Ratio** — the single equation that connects the known
   baseline and angle to the unknown width.
5. **Solving for River Width** — a fully worked numeric example
   (baseline 40 m, angle 30°, width ≈ 23.1 m).
6. **Verification via a Second Baseline** — confirming the answer with an
   independent triangle, modelling good scientific practice.

Each concept is followed by exactly one interaction before the next concept
begins, so learners apply an idea immediately after meeting it.

## Estimated Duration

**8 minutes 00 seconds** of video, plus approximately **4–6 minutes** of
interaction and reflection time (paused segments are excluded from video
runtime). Total seat time: **12–14 minutes**.

## Difficulty

**Intermediate** — builds directly on prior trigonometric-ratio knowledge;
introduces no new formulas beyond the tangent ratio, but requires students
to translate a word scenario into a diagram, which is the primary source of
difficulty.
