# Common Misconceptions

## 1. "A pair always has a unique solution"

Students may assume every pair of linear equations has exactly one solution. Clarify that pairs can be inconsistent or dependent.

## 2. Sign errors in substitution

Students often make sign errors when expressing one variable in terms of another or substituting negative values. Encourage bracket use.

## 3. Confusing parallel and coincident conditions

Students may think $\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} = \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}$ always means parallel. Remind them to check $\dfrac{c_1}{c_2}$.

## 4. Forgetting to verify

Students may not substitute the final values back into both equations. Emphasise verification as a final step.

## 5. Incorrectly forming equations from word problems

Students may use only one variable or form only one equation. Provide structured templates.

## 6. Misapplying cross-multiplication

Students may mix up the positions of $a$, $b$, and $c$ in the cross-multiplication formula. Write the formula clearly each time.

## 7. Not converting back after substitution in reducible equations

After solving for $u$ and $v$, students may forget to find $x$ and $y$. Remind them to reverse the substitution.
