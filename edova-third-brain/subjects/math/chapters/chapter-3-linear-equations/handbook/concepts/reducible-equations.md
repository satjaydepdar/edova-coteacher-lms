# Equations Reducible to a Pair of Linear Equations

## When Equations Are Not Linear

Some equations may not be linear in $x$ and $y$ at first, but they can be reduced to a linear pair by a suitable substitution.

## Common Form

Equations of the form:

$$\frac{a_1}{x} + \frac{b_1}{y} = c_1$$
$$\frac{a_2}{x} + \frac{b_2}{y} = c_2$$

can be reduced to linear form by substituting:

$$u = \frac{1}{x}, \quad v = \frac{1}{y}$$

This gives:

$$a_1u + b_1v = c_1$$
$$a_2u + b_2v = c_2$$

which is a linear pair in $u$ and $v$.

## Example

Solve:

$$\frac{2}{x} + \frac{3}{y} = 13$$
$$\frac{5}{x} - \frac{4}{y} = -2$$

Let $u = \dfrac{1}{x}$ and $v = \dfrac{1}{y}$. Then:

$$2u + 3v = 13 \quad \text{...(1)}$$
$$5u - 4v = -2 \quad \text{...(2)}$$

Multiply equation (1) by 4 and equation (2) by 3:

$$8u + 12v = 52 \quad \text{...(3)}$$
$$15u - 12v = -6 \quad \text{...(4)}$$

Add equations (3) and (4):

$$23u = 46 \Rightarrow u = 2$$

Substitute in equation (1):

$$2(2) + 3v = 13 \Rightarrow 3v = 9 \Rightarrow v = 3$$

Since $u = \dfrac{1}{x}$ and $v = \dfrac{1}{y}$:

$$x = \frac{1}{2}, \quad y = \frac{1}{3}$$
