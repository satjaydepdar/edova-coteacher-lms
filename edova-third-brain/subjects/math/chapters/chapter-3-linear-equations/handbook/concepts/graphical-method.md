# Graphical Method of Solution

## Representing Equations as Lines

Each linear equation in two variables represents a straight line on a graph. To draw a line, we need only two points that satisfy the equation.

## Nature of Solutions

When we draw the graphs of both equations on the same axes, one of three cases occurs:

| Graphical Representation | Algebraic Interpretation | Number of Solutions |
|---|---|---|
| The two lines intersect at one point. | Consistent pair | Exactly one (unique) |
| The two lines are parallel. | Inconsistent pair | No solution |
| The two lines coincide. | Dependent (consistent) pair | Infinitely many |

## Coefficient Ratio Test

For the pair:

$$a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0$$
$$a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0$$

- If $\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} \neq \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}$, the lines intersect.
- If $\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} = \dfrac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}$, the lines are parallel.
- If $\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} = \dfrac{b_1}{b_2} = \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}$, the lines coincide.

## Example

Consider $x + 3y = 6$ and $2x - 3y = 12$.

Two points on $x + 3y = 6$: $(0, 2)$ and $(6, 0)$.
Two points on $2x - 3y = 12$: $(0, -4)$ and $(3, -2)$.

The lines intersect at $(6, 0)$. Therefore, the unique solution is $x = 6$, $y = 0$.
