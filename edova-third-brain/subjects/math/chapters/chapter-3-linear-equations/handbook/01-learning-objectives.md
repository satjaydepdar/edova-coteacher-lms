## Knowledge (Remember)

- Define a linear equation in two variables and a pair of linear equations in two variables.
- State the general form $a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0$ and $a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0$.
- Recall the terms consistent, inconsistent, and dependent pair of linear equations.
- List the algebraic methods: substitution, elimination, and cross-multiplication.

## Understanding (Comprehend)

- Explain why the graph of a linear equation in two variables is a straight line.
- Interpret the graphical meaning of intersecting, parallel, and coincident lines.
- Relate the ratios $\dfrac{a_1}{a_2}$, $\dfrac{b_1}{b_2}$, and $\dfrac{c_1}{c_2}$ to consistency and dependence.
- Describe how a non-linear pair of equations can be reduced to a linear pair.

## Application (Apply)

- Solve a pair of linear equations graphically and algebraically.
- Apply the substitution, elimination, and cross-multiplication methods accurately.
- Solve word problems by forming a pair of linear equations.
- Reduce equations reducible to linear form and solve them.

## Analysis (Analyse)

- Compare graphical and algebraic methods for solving a pair of linear equations.
- Determine the nature of solutions by comparing coefficients.
- Identify errors in algebraic steps and correct them.

## Evaluation (Evaluate)

- Judge which algebraic method is most efficient for a given pair of equations.
- Evaluate the correctness of a solution by verification.
- Decide whether a real-life situation can be modelled by a pair of linear equations.

## Creation (Create)

- Formulate word problems that lead to a pair of linear equations.
- Design a flowchart for choosing the appropriate method of solution.
- Create a real-life case study involving two unknown quantities.
