# Case Study Example

## Taxi Charges in a City

The taxi charges in a city consist of a fixed charge together with a charge for the distance covered. For a distance of 10 km, the charge paid is ₹105. For a journey of 15 km, the charge paid is ₹155.

### Information

- Distance 1 = 10 km, Charge 1 = ₹105
- Distance 2 = 15 km, Charge 2 = ₹155

### Questions

1. What are the fixed charge and the charge per km?
2. How much does a person have to pay for travelling a distance of 25 km?

### Solutions

Let fixed charge = ₹$x$ and charge per km = ₹$y$.

For 10 km:

$$x + 10y = 105 \quad \text{...(1)}$$

For 15 km:

$$x + 15y = 155 \quad \text{...(2)}$$

**1. Fixed charge and charge per km**

Subtract equation (1) from equation (2):

$$5y = 50$$
$$y = 10$$

Substitute in equation (1):

$$x + 10(10) = 105$$
$$x = 5$$

Fixed charge = ₹5, charge per km = ₹10.

**2. Charge for 25 km**

$$\text{Total charge} = x + 25y = 5 + 25(10) = 255$$

The person has to pay ₹255 for 25 km.

### Reflection

This case study shows how a pair of linear equations can model real-life billing systems. Similar problems can be framed for electricity bills, water bills, or mobile recharge plans.
