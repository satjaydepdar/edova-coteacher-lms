# Lesson 1

## Chapter 3: Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables
### Topic: Introduction and Graphical Method of Solution (NCERT Sections 3.1 and 3.2)

**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Target Audience:** Class X Students

---

### 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
- Define a pair of linear equations in two variables.
- Represent a pair of linear equations graphically.
- Identify whether a pair is consistent, inconsistent, or dependent from its graph.

---

### 2. Key Concepts

- A linear equation in two variables: $ax + by + c = 0$.
- A pair of linear equations in two variables:
  $$a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0$$
  $$a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0$$
- Graphical interpretation:
  - Intersecting lines → unique solution (consistent).
  - Parallel lines → no solution (inconsistent).
  - Coincident lines → infinitely many solutions (dependent consistent).

---

### 3. Teaching-Learning Process

#### Introduction (10 mins)
- **Hook:** Akhila at a fair — rides on the Giant Wheel and Hoopla games. Form two equations.
- **Recall:** Linear equations in two variables from Class IX.
- **Define:** Introduce the general form of a pair of linear equations.

#### Main Activity (25 mins)
- **Graphing:** Plot two lines on the same axes.
- **Example 1:** Solve $x + 3y = 6$ and $2x - 3y = 12$ graphically.
- **Example 2:** Show that $5x - 8y + 1 = 0$ and $3x - \dfrac{24}{5}y + \dfrac{3}{5} = 0$ have infinitely many solutions.
- **Example 3:** Word problem on pants and skirts solved graphically.
- **Student Practice:** Begin NCERT Exercise 3.1 in class.

#### Conclusion (10 mins)
- Summarise the three cases for graphical solutions.
- Introduce the coefficient ratio test for predicting the nature of solutions.
- Assign remaining Exercise 3.1 questions as homework.

---

### 4. Assessment

- **Exit Ticket:** Without graphing, predict the nature of $x + 2y - 4 = 0$ and $2x + 4y - 12 = 0$. Justify using ratios.
