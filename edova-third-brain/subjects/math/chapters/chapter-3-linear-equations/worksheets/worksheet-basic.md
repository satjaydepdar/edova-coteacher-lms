## Section A: Graphical Method (Based on Exercise 3.1)

**Q1.** Fill in the blanks:

(i) A pair of linear equations which has no solution is called an ___________ pair of linear equations.

(ii) A pair of linear equations which has infinitely many solutions is called a ___________ pair of linear equations.

(iii) If the lines representing a pair of linear equations intersect at a point, the pair is ___________.

(iv) For the pair $a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0$ and $a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0$, the lines are parallel if $\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} = \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}$ ___________ $\dfrac{c_1}{c_2}$. (equal / not equal)

**Q2.** Without drawing the graph, determine whether the following pairs of linear equations intersect, are parallel, or coincident:

(i) $5x - 4y + 8 = 0$ and $7x + 6y - 9 = 0$

(ii) $9x + 3y + 12 = 0$ and $18x + 6y + 24 = 0$

(iii) $6x - 3y + 10 = 0$ and $2x - y + 9 = 0$

**Q3.** Solve the following pair of equations by the substitution method:

$$x + y = 14$$
$$x - y = 4$$

## Section C: Identify the Outcome

**Q5.** In each case, state whether the pair has a unique solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions:

(i) $2x - 3y = 8$ and $4x - 6y = 9$

(ii) $5x - 3y = 11$ and $-10x + 6y = -22$

(iii) $3x + 2y = 5$ and $2x - 3y = 7$

---

<details>
<summary><strong>Answer Key</strong></summary>

**Q1.**
(i) inconsistent
(ii) dependent
(iii) consistent (with unique solution)
(iv) not equal

**Q2.**
(i) $\dfrac{5}{7} \neq \dfrac{-4}{6}$, so the lines **intersect** at a point.

(ii) $\dfrac{9}{18} = \dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{12}{24} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so the lines are **coincident**.

(iii) $\dfrac{6}{2} = \dfrac{-3}{-1} = 3$ but $\dfrac{10}{9} \neq 3$, so the lines are **parallel**.

**Q3.** From the second equation, $x = y + 4$. Substituting in the first equation:

$$(y + 4) + y = 14 \Rightarrow 2y = 10 \Rightarrow y = 5$$

Then $x = 5 + 4 = 9$. So the solution is $x = 9,\ y = 5$.

**Q4.** $x = \dfrac{9 - 3y}{2}$

**Q5.**
(i) $\dfrac{2}{4} = \dfrac{-3}{-6} \neq \dfrac{8}{9}$, so **no solution** (inconsistent).

(ii) $\dfrac{5}{-10} = \dfrac{-3}{6} = \dfrac{11}{-22} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, so **infinitely many solutions** (dependent).

(iii) $\dfrac{3}{2} \neq \dfrac{2}{-3}$, so **unique solution** (consistent).
