# Long Answer Questions

**Q1.** Solve graphically: $x + 3y = 6$ and $2x - 3y = 12$.

**Q2.** Show graphically that $5x - 8y + 1 = 0$ and $3x - \dfrac{24}{5}y + \dfrac{3}{5} = 0$ have infinitely many solutions.

**Q3.** Solve by substitution: $7x - 15y = 2$ and $x + 2y = 3$.

**Q4.** Aftab tells his daughter, "Seven years ago, I was seven times as old as you were then. Also, three years from now, I shall be three times as old as you will be." Find their present ages.

**Q5.** Show that $2x + 3y = 9$ and $4x + 6y = 18$ have infinitely many solutions. Explain why a unique cost cannot be found.

**Q6.** Show that the pair $x + 2y - 4 = 0$ and $2x + 4y - 12 = 0$ has no solution.

**Q7.** The ratio of incomes of two persons is $9 : 7$ and the ratio of their expenditures is $4 : 3$. If each saves ₹2000 per month, find their monthly incomes.

**Q8.** The sum of a two-digit number and the number obtained by reversing its digits is 66. If the digits differ by 2, find the number. How many such numbers are there?

**Q9.** Draw the graphs of $x - y + 1 = 0$ and $3x + 2y - 12 = 0$. Determine the coordinates of the vertices of the triangle formed by these lines and the $x$-axis.

**Q10.** Solve by elimination: $3x - 5y - 4 = 0$ and $9x = 2y + 7$.

---

## Answers

**A1.** Two points on $x + 3y = 6$: $(0, 2)$ and $(6, 0)$. Two points on $2x - 3y = 12$: $(0, -4)$ and $(3, -2)$. The lines intersect at $(6, 0)$. Solution: $x = 6$, $y = 0$.

**A2.** Multiplying the second equation by $\dfrac{5}{3}$ gives $5x - 8y + 1 = 0$, which is identical to the first. The lines coincide, so there are infinitely many solutions.

**A3.** From $x + 2y = 3$, $x = 3 - 2y$. Substituting in $7x - 15y = 2$: $7(3 - 2y) - 15y = 2 \Rightarrow 21 - 29y = 2 \Rightarrow y = \dfrac{19}{29}$. Then $x = \dfrac{49}{29}$.

**A4.** Let Aftab's age be $s$ and daughter's age be $t$. Equations: $s - 7t + 42 = 0$ and $s - 3t = 6$. Solving, $t = 12$ and $s = 42$. Aftab is 42 years old and his daughter is 12 years old.

**A5.** Dividing the second equation by 2 gives $2x + 3y = 9$, same as the first. All ratios are equal, so the lines coincide and there are infinitely many solutions. Hence no unique cost exists.

**A6.** $\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{2}{4} \neq \dfrac{-4}{-12}$. Since $\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} = \dfrac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}$, the pair is inconsistent and has no solution.

**A7.** Let incomes be ₹$9x$ and ₹$7x$, and expenditures be ₹$4y$ and ₹$3y$. Equations: $9x - 4y = 2000$ and $7x - 3y = 2000$. Solving, $x = 2000$ and $y = 4000$. Incomes are ₹18,000 and ₹14,000.

**A8.** Let the ten's digit be $x$ and unit's digit be $y$. Then $x + y = 6$ and either $x - y = 2$ or $y - x = 2$. The numbers are 42 and 24. There are two such numbers.

**A9.** The lines meet the $x$-axis at $(-1, 0)$ and $(4, 0)$, and each other at $(2, 3)$. The vertices of the triangle are $(-1, 0)$, $(4, 0)$, and $(2, 3)$.

**A10.** From $9x = 2y + 7$, we get $9x - 2y = 7$. Multiply the first equation by 3: $9x - 15y = 12$. Subtract: $13y = -5 \Rightarrow y = -\dfrac{5}{13}$. Then $x = \dfrac{9}{13}$.
