# Assertion-Reason Questions

For each question, choose the correct option:

(a) Both Assertion and Reason are true and Reason is the correct explanation of Assertion.
(b) Both Assertion and Reason are true but Reason is not the correct explanation of Assertion.
(c) Assertion is true but Reason is false.
(d) Assertion is false but Reason is true.

**Q1.** **Assertion:** The list $2, 4, 8, 16, \ldots$ is an arithmetic progression.

**Reason:** A list of numbers is an AP if the difference between any two consecutive terms is constant.

**Q2.** **Assertion:** The 10th term of the AP $2, 7, 12, 17, \ldots$ is 47.

**Reason:** The $n$th term of an AP is given by $a_n = a + (n-1)d$.

**Q3.** **Assertion:** The sum of the first $n$ natural numbers is $\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$.

**Reason:** The natural numbers $1, 2, 3, \ldots, n$ form an AP with first term 1 and common difference 1.

**Q4.** **Assertion:** If $S_n = 3n^2 + 5n$, the AP has a common difference of 6.

**Reason:** The common difference of an AP can always be found directly from the coefficient of $n$ in $S_n$ without further calculation.

---

## Answers

**A1.** (d) Assertion is false — the list has ratio 2, not a constant difference, so it is not an AP. Reason (the definition of an AP) is true.

**A2.** (a) Both true; Reason correctly explains how Assertion is calculated ($2 + 9 \times 5 = 47$).

**A3.** (a) Both true; Reason correctly explains why the sum formula applies.

**A4.** (c) Assertion is true ($d = 6$, verified by direct calculation from $S_n$), but Reason is false — the common difference is **not** simply the coefficient of $n$; it must be derived using $a_1 = S_1$ and $a_2 = S_2 - S_1$.
