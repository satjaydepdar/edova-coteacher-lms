## Short Answer

1. AP: each term is obtained from the preceding one by adding a fixed common difference.
2. $d = -3$ for $15, 12, 9, 6, \ldots$
3. $a_{15} = 91$ for $7, 13, 19, 25, \ldots$
4. 301 is **not** a term of $5, 11, 17, 23, \ldots$
5. Sum of first 10 multiples of 5 $= 275$.
6. General form: $a, a+d, a+2d, \ldots$
7. $2 + 4 + \ldots + 40 = 420$.
8. $-81$ is the 35th term of $21, 18, 15, \ldots$
9. $a_{25} = -68$ for $a_n = 7 - 3n$.
10. $a_n = S_n - S_{n-1}$.

## Long Answer

1. $S_{22} = -979$ for AP $8, 3, -2, \ldots$
2. 78 is the 16th term of $3, 8, 13, 18, \ldots$; $S_{16} = 648$.
3. AP with 3rd term 16, and 7th exceeds 5th by 12: $4, 10, 16, 22, 28, \ldots$
4. Sum of first 40 positive multiples of 6 $= 4920$.
5. 12 terms of $9, 17, 25, \ldots$ give a sum of 636.
6. For $S_n = 3n^2 + 5n$: $a = 8$, $d = 6$, $a_n = 6n + 2$.
7. AP with $a_4 + a_8 = 24$ and $a_6 + a_{10} = 44$: $-13, -8, -3, 2, 7, \ldots$
8. Sum of two-digit multiples of 4 $= 1188$.

## Marking Guidance

- **MCQs:** 1 mark each; no partial credit.
- **Short answer:** 2 marks each; 1 mark for method, 1 mark for final answer.
- **Long answer:** 3–5 marks each; credit correct formula selection and clarity of steps.
- **Assertion-Reason:** 1 mark each; both Assertion and Reason must be evaluated.
- **Case study / competency:** 3–4 marks each; reward correct modelling of the situation as an AP and correct interpretation of the answer.
- **Challenge problems:** 4–5 marks each; partial credit for correct intermediate algebraic steps.
