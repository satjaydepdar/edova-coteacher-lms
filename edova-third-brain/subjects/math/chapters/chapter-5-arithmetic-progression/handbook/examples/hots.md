# Higher Order Thinking Skills (HOTS) Examples

## Example 7: Term from the End

Find the 11th term from the last term of the AP $10, 7, 4, \ldots, -62$.

**Solution:**

Here, $a = 10$, $d = -3$, and $l = -62$.

First, find the total number of terms:

$$-62 = 10 + (n - 1)(-3)$$

$$-72 = -3(n - 1)$$

$$n - 1 = 24 \Rightarrow n = 25$$

The 11th term from the last is the $(25 - 11 + 1)$th term, i.e., the 15th term.

$$a_{15} = 10 + (15 - 1)(-3) = 10 - 42 = -32$$

So, the 11th term from the last is $-32$.

## Example 8: Sum and Term Combined

If the sum of the first 14 terms of an AP is 1050 and its first term is 10, find the 20th term.

**Solution:**

Here, $S_{14} = 1050$, $n = 14$, $a = 10$.

$$S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d]$$

$$1050 = \frac{14}{2}[20 + 13d]$$

$$1050 = 7(20 + 13d)$$

$$150 = 20 + 13d$$

$$13d = 130 \Rightarrow d = 10$$

Now, find the 20th term:

$$a_{20} = 10 + (20 - 1) \times 10 = 10 + 190 = 200$$

The 20th term is $200$.

## Example 9: Two Possible Values of $n$

How many terms of the AP $24, 21, 18, \ldots$ must be taken so that their sum is 78?

**Solution:**

Here, $a = 24$, $d = -3$, $S_n = 78$.

$$78 = \frac{n}{2}[48 + (n - 1)(-3)]$$

$$156 = n(51 - 3n)$$

$$156 = 51n - 3n^2$$

$$3n^2 - 51n + 156 = 0$$

$$n^2 - 17n + 52 = 0$$

$$(n - 4)(n - 13) = 0$$

$$n = 4 \text{ or } 13$$

Both values are admissible. So, the sum is 78 when 4 terms or 13 terms are taken.
