# Chapter 5: Arithmetic Progressions

## What Students Will Learn

This chapter introduces students to number patterns called sequences, with special focus on arithmetic progressions. Students will:

1. Identify sequences in which each successive term is obtained by adding a fixed number.
2. Define an arithmetic progression (AP) and its common difference.
3. Find the $n$th term (general term) of an AP.
4. Find the sum of the first $n$ terms of an AP.
5. Solve real-life problems modelled by APs such as salaries, savings, penalties, and arrangements.

## Key Examples from the Textbook

- Example 1: Find $a$ and $d$ for the AP $\dfrac{3}{2}, \dfrac{1}{2}, -\dfrac{1}{2}, -\dfrac{3}{2}, \ldots$
- Example 2: Identify which lists form APs and write the next two terms.
- Example 3: Find the 10th term of the AP $2, 7, 12, \ldots$
- Example 4: Which term of $21, 18, 15, \ldots$ is $-81$? Is any term 0?
- Example 5: Determine the AP whose 3rd term is 5 and 7th term is 9.
- Example 6: Check whether 301 is a term of $5, 11, 17, 23, \ldots$
- Example 7: How many two-digit numbers are divisible by 3?
- Example 8: Find the 11th term from the last term of $10, 7, 4, \ldots, -62$.
- Example 9: Simple interest on ₹1000 at 8% per year forms an AP.
- Example 10: Number of rose plants in rows forms a decreasing AP.
- Example 11: Find the sum of the first 22 terms of $8, 3, -2, \ldots$
- Example 12: Given $S_{14} = 1050$ and $a = 10$, find the 20th term.
- Example 13: How many terms of $24, 21, 18, \ldots$ have sum 78?
- Example 14: Sum of first 1000 positive integers; sum of first $n$ positive integers.
- Example 15: Sum when the $n$th term is given as $a_n = 3 + 2n$.
- Example 16: TV production problem using AP.

## Competency Focus

- **Problem Solving:** Translate real-life situations into AP terminology.
- **Reasoning:** Justify whether a sequence is an AP and choose the correct formula.
- **Communication:** Present solutions clearly with the formula stated and values substituted.
