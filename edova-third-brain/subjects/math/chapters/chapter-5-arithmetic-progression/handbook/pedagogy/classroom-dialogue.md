# Classroom Dialogue

## Opening Discussion

**Teacher:** "Reena's salary starts at ₹8000 and increases by ₹500 every year. Can you write her salaries for the first few years?"

**Student:** "₹8000, ₹8500, ₹9000, ₹9500, \ldots"

**Teacher:** "What pattern do you notice?"

**Student:** "Each year, the salary increases by ₹500."

**Teacher:** "This is an example of an arithmetic progression. Today we will learn about such patterns."

## On Identifying an AP

**Teacher:** "How do you check whether a list of numbers is an AP?"

**Student:** "Find the difference between consecutive terms and see if it is always the same."

**Teacher:** "What is that constant difference called?"

**Student:** "The common difference, $d$."

## On the nth Term Formula

**Teacher:** "Why is the formula $a_n = a + (n - 1)d$ and not $a + nd$?"

**Student:** "Because the first term is already $a$, so we add $d$ only $(n - 1)$ times to reach the $n$th term."

## On the Sum Formula

**Teacher:** "How did Gauss find the sum $1 + 2 + 3 + \ldots + 100$ quickly?"

**Student:** "He paired the first and last numbers, the second and second-last, and so on. Each pair sums to 101, and there are 50 pairs."

## Closing Reflection

**Teacher:** "Where in real life do we see arithmetic progressions?"

**Student:** "Salary increments, savings plans, penalties, stair steps, and production plans."
