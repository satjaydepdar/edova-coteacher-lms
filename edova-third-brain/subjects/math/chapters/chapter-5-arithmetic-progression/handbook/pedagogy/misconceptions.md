# Common Misconceptions

## 1. "The common difference is always positive"

Students often assume $d$ must be positive. Clarify that $d$ can be positive, negative, or zero.

## 2. "$n$th term means multiply by $n$"

Some students write $a_n = a + nd$ instead of $a_n = a + (n - 1)d$. Emphasise that the first term already corresponds to $n = 1$.

## 3. Confusing term number with term value

Students may mix up the position $n$ with the value $a_n$. Use explicit labels: "the 10th term" vs. "the value of the 10th term".

## 4. "Any list with a pattern is an AP"

Not every pattern is an AP. Sequences like $1, 4, 9, 16, \ldots$ or $2, 4, 8, 16, \ldots$ have patterns but are not APs because the difference between consecutive terms is not constant.

## 5. Sign errors with negative $d$

When $d$ is negative, students often make sign mistakes. Encourage careful substitution and verification.

## 6. "The sum formula always needs the last term"

Students may think they cannot find the sum without $l$. Show that $S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d]$ works when $l$ is unknown.

## 7. Forgetting to check that $n$ is a positive integer

When checking if a number is a term, students may accept a fractional or negative value of $n$. Remind them that $n$ must be a positive integer.
