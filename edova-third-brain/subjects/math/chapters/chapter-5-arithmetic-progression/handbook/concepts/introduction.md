# Introduction to Sequences

## What is a Sequence?

A **sequence** is an ordered list of numbers arranged according to a rule. Each number in the sequence is called a **term**.

Examples of sequences from daily life and nature:

- Petals of a sunflower, holes of a honeycomb, grains on a maize cob.
- Salary with a fixed annual increment: ₹8000, ₹8500, ₹9000, \ldots
- Lengths of ladder rungs decreasing uniformly: 45, 43, 41, 39, \ldots
- Amount in a savings scheme growing by a fixed factor (not an AP, but a sequence).
- Number of unit squares in squares of side 1, 2, 3, \ldots units: $1^2, 2^2, 3^2, \ldots$
- Savings increasing by ₹50 every year: ₹100, ₹150, ₹200, ₹250, \ldots

## Patterns in Sequences

In the examples above, different patterns are observed:

- Some sequences are formed by **adding a fixed number**.
- Some are formed by **multiplying by a fixed number**.
- Some are formed by **squaring consecutive integers**.

In this chapter, we focus on sequences in which each succeeding term is obtained by **adding a fixed number** to the preceding term.

## Real-Life Relevance

Arithmetic progressions appear in many real-life situations:

- Fixed annual salary increments
- Uniform decrease in rung lengths of a ladder
- Fixed periodic savings
- Penalties increasing by a fixed amount each day
