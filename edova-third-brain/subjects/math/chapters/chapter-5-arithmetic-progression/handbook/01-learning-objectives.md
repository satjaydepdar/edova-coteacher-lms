## Knowledge (Remember)

- Define a sequence and an arithmetic progression (AP).
- State the meaning of first term ($a$) and common difference ($d$).
- Recall the formula for the $n$th term of an AP: $a_n = a + (n - 1)d$.
- Recall the formula for the sum of the first $n$ terms: $S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d]$ or $S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}(a + l)$.

## Understanding (Comprehend)

- Explain why the difference between consecutive terms is constant in an AP.
- Distinguish between finite and infinite arithmetic progressions.
- Interpret the $n$th term formula as a linear relationship in $n$.
- Explain how the sum formula is derived by pairing terms from opposite ends.

## Application (Apply)

- Identify whether a given list of numbers forms an AP and find its common difference.
- Find a specific term, the number of terms, or the common difference when other values are given.
- Calculate the sum of a given number of terms of an AP.
- Solve real-life problems such as salaries with fixed increments, penalties, savings, and arrangements.

## Analysis (Analyse)

- Determine which formula ($a_n$ or $S_n$) is appropriate for a given problem.
- Analyse word problems to extract $a$, $d$, $n$, $a_n$, or $S_n$.
- Compare two APs using their common differences and corresponding terms.

## Evaluation (Evaluate)

- Judge whether a given sequence is an AP and justify the answer.
- Verify solutions by substituting back into the original conditions.
- Evaluate different methods of finding the sum of an AP.

## Creation (Create)

- Design real-life situations that can be modelled as an AP.
- Construct APs given specific conditions (e.g., 3rd term and 7th term).
- Develop a short project or poster summarising AP formulas and applications.
