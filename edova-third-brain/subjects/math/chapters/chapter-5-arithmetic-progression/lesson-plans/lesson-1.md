# Lesson 1

## Chapter 5: Arithmetic Progressions
### Topic: Introduction to Sequences and Arithmetic Progressions (NCERT Sections 5.1–5.2)

**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Target Audience:** Class X Students

---

### 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
- Define a sequence and recognise numerical patterns.
- Define an arithmetic progression (AP) and its common difference.
- Determine whether a given list of numbers is an AP.
- Write the general form of an AP.

---

### 2. Key Concepts

- Sequence: an ordered list of numbers following a rule.
- Arithmetic Progression (AP): a sequence in which the difference between consecutive terms is constant.
- Common difference: $d = a_{k+1} - a_k$, the same for every $k$.
- General form of an AP: $a,\ a+d,\ a+2d,\ a+3d,\ \ldots$

---

### 3. Teaching-Learning Process

#### Introduction (10 mins)
- **Hook:** Present a taxi fare situation — fare for the first km is ₹15, and each additional km costs ₹8. List the fares for 1, 2, 3, 4 km on the board.
- **Recall:** Discuss patterns students already know (multiplication tables, calendar dates).
- **Define:** Introduce the term "sequence," then narrow to sequences with a constant difference — arithmetic progressions.

#### Main Activity (25 mins)
- **Identify APs:** Give students several lists (e.g., $2, 7, 12, 17, \ldots$; $1, 3, 6, 10, \ldots$; $10, 7, 4, 1, \ldots$) and have them check for a constant difference.
- **Formalise:** Derive the general form $a, a+d, a+2d, \ldots$ from the taxi fare example ($a = 15$, $d = 8$).
- **Practice:** Find $d$ for several given APs, including ones with negative common differences.
- **Discuss:** Why is $1, 4, 9, 16, \ldots$ (squares) *not* an AP?

#### Conclusion (10 mins)
- Summarise the definition of an AP, the meaning of $d$, and the general form.
- Assign: identify which of a given set of lists are APs, and find $a$ and $d$ for each that is.

---

### 4. Assessment

- **Exit Ticket:** Is the list $1, 3, 6, 10, \ldots$ an AP? Justify. Find the common difference of the AP $100, 90, 80, 70, \ldots$
