## Case Study 1: Auditorium Seating

A theatre has 20 seats in the first row, 22 seats in the second row, 24 seats in the third row, and so on, each row having 2 more seats than the previous one.

**Q1.** How many seats are there in the 15th row?

**Q2.** How many total seats are there in the first 15 rows?

**Q3.** Which row is the first to have at least 50 seats?

---

## Case Study 2: Stacking Logs

200 logs are stacked so that the bottom row has 20 logs, the next row has 19 logs, and so on, decreasing by 1 log per row.

**Q4.** In how many rows are the 200 logs placed?

**Q5.** How many logs are in the top row?

**Q6.** Why is only one of the two mathematical solutions for the number of rows valid here?

---

<details>
<summary><strong>Answer Key</strong></summary>

**Case Study 1**

**A1.** $a = 20$, $d = 2$. $a_{15} = 20 + 14(2) = 48$ seats.

**A2.** $S_{15} = \frac{15}{2}[40 + 14(2)] = 510$ seats.

**A3.** $20 + (n-1)2 \geq 50 \Rightarrow n \geq 16$. The 16th row.

**Case Study 2**

**A4.** $a = 20$, $d = -1$, $S_n = 200 \Rightarrow n^2 - 41n + 400 = 0 \Rightarrow n = 16$ or $25$. **16 rows**.

**A5.** Top row: $a_{16} = 20 - 15 = 5$ logs.

**A6.** $n = 25$ is rejected because the number of logs per row cannot go negative — the stack runs out of logs before reaching a 25th row.
