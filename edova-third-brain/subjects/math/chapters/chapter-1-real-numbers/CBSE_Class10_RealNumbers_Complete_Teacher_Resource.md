# 1. Learning Outcomes

-   Classify real numbers.
-   Apply Euclid's Division Algorithm.
-   Use the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.
-   Find HCF and LCM.
-   Determine terminating and recurring decimals.
-   Construct proofs for irrational numbers.
-   Solve real-life applications involving HCF and LCM.

# 4. 40-Minute Lesson Plan

-   Introduction (5 min)
-   Euclid's Division Lemma (10 min)
-   Fundamental Theorem (10 min)
-   HCF & LCM Practice (10 min)
-   Exit Ticket (5 min)

# 5. Teacher Notes

-   Emphasize the difference between Euclid's Lemma and Algorithm.
-   Reduce fractions before checking decimal expansion.
-   Demonstrate prime factorization using factor trees.
-   Use real-life examples such as packaging and scheduling.

# 6. Student Notes

-   a = bq + r, where 0 ≤ r \< b
-   Last non-zero remainder = HCF
-   Prime factorization is unique.
-   LCM × HCF = Product (for two numbers)
-   Denominator with only 2 and/or 5 → Terminating decimal.

# 7. Board-Style Questions

1.  State Euclid's Division Lemma.
2.  Find HCF of 455 and 42.
3.  Find HCF and LCM of 72 and 120.
4.  Determine whether 21/160 has a terminating decimal.
5.  Prove √7 is irrational.
6.  Solve a grouping problem using HCF.

# 8. Competency-Based Questions

-   Explain why 21/160 has a terminating decimal.
-   Two bells ring every 18 and 24 minutes. When will they ring together
    again?
-   Evaluate: √49 is irrational.
-   Prove 3√2 is irrational.
-   A bakery packs 420 and 315 muffins into identical boxes. Find the
    maximum number of boxes.
