# Teacher Notes -- Chapter 1: Real Numbers (CBSE Class 10)

## Learning Outcomes

-   Understand Euclid's Division Lemma and Algorithm.
-   Find HCF using Euclid's Algorithm.
-   Apply the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.
-   Find HCF and LCM using prime factorization.
-   Determine whether a rational number has a terminating or recurring
    decimal expansion.

## Prerequisite Knowledge

-   Factors and multiples
-   Prime and composite numbers
-   Fractions
-   Integers
-   Division

# 1. Real Numbers

Real numbers include: - Natural Numbers (N) - Whole Numbers (W) -
Integers (Z) - Rational Numbers (Q) - Irrational Numbers

  Number      Rational   Irrational
  ---------- ---------- ------------
  3/7            ✓      
  √4 (=2)        ✓      
  √5                         ✓
  π                          ✓
  0.25           ✓      
  0.333...       ✓      

# 2. Euclid's Division Lemma

For positive integers **a** and **b**,

> **a = bq + r**, where **0 ≤ r \< b**

-   a = Dividend
-   b = Divisor
-   q = Quotient
-   r = Remainder

Example:

38 = 5 × 7 + 3

# 3. Euclid's Division Algorithm

Example: Find HCF of 455 and 42

455 = 42 × 10 + 35

42 = 35 × 1 + 7

35 = 7 × 5 + 0

**HCF = 7**

> The last non-zero remainder is the HCF.

# 4. Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every composite number can be expressed as a **unique product of prime
numbers**.

Example:

60 = 2² × 3 × 5

# 5. HCF and LCM

Example:

24 = 2³ × 3

36 = 2² × 3²

HCF = 2² × 3 = 12

LCM = 2³ × 3² = 72

For two positive integers:

> **LCM × HCF = Product of the two numbers**

# 6. Decimal Expansion

A rational number p/q (in lowest form):

-   **Terminating decimal:** denominator has only 2 and/or 5 as prime
    factors.
-   **Recurring decimal:** denominator has any prime factor other than 2
    or 5.

Examples:

  Fraction   Decimal Type
  ---------- --------------
  1/2        Terminating
  3/8        Terminating
  1/3        Recurring
  2/7        Recurring

# Common Mistakes

-   Forgetting 0 ≤ r \< b
-   Stopping Euclid's Algorithm too early
-   Incorrect prime factorization
-   Not reducing fractions before checking decimal expansion

# Board Exam Tips

-   Show every step in Euclid's Algorithm.
-   Reduce fractions to lowest terms first.
-   Memorize the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.
-   Practice HCF/LCM questions regularly.
