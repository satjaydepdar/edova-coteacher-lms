## Section A: Higher-Order Relationships

**Q1.** If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the zeroes of $x^2 - px + q$, find the value of $\alpha^3 + \beta^3$ in terms of $p$ and $q$.

**Q2.** If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the zeroes of $x^2 - 5x + 6$, form a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes are $\alpha + 1$ and $\beta + 1$.

---

## Section B: Polynomials with Special Conditions

**Q3.** If one zero of the polynomial $3x^2 - 8x + k$ is the reciprocal of the other, find the value of $k$.

**Q4.** For what value of $k$ does the polynomial $x^2 - 4x + k$ have equal zeroes?

**Q5.** Find all the zeroes of $x^4 - 13x^2 + 36$.

---

## Section C: Application

**Q6.** A ball thrown upwards follows the path $h(t) = -t^2 + 4t$, where $h$ is height in metres and $t$ is time in seconds. After how many seconds does the ball hit the ground?

---

<details>
<summary><strong>Answer Key</strong></summary>

**Q1.** $\alpha^3 + \beta^3 = (\alpha + \beta)^3 - 3\alpha\beta(\alpha + \beta) = p^3 - 3pq$.

**Q2.** For $x^2 - 5x + 6$: $\alpha + \beta = 5$, $\alpha\beta = 6$.

New sum $= (\alpha + 1) + (\beta + 1) = 7$.

New product $= (\alpha + 1)(\beta + 1) = \alpha\beta + \alpha + \beta + 1 = 6 + 5 + 1 = 12$.

New polynomial: $x^2 - 7x + 12$.

**Q3.** Let zeroes be $\alpha$ and $\frac{1}{\alpha}$. Product $= 1 = \frac{k}{3}$, so $k = 3$.

**Q4.** For equal zeroes, discriminant $= 0$: $16 - 4k = 0 \Rightarrow k = 4$.

**Q5.** Let $y = x^2$. Then $y^2 - 13y + 36 = 0 \Rightarrow (y - 4)(y - 9) = 0$.

So $x^2 = 4$ or $x^2 = 9$. Zeroes are $\pm 2$ and $\pm 3$.

**Q6.** $h(t) = -t^2 + 4t = -t(t - 4) = 0$. Hence $t = 0$ or $t = 4$. The ball hits the ground after $4$ seconds.

</details>
