## Short Answer

1. **Degree of a polynomial:** highest power of the variable.
2. Zero of $5x + 10$ is $x = -2$.
3. For $p(x) = x^2 - 3x - 4$: $p(0) = -4$, $p(-1) = 0$.
4. $2x^3 - 5x^2 + x - 1$ is a **cubic** polynomial.
5. Zeroes of $x^2 - 9$ are $3$ and $-3$.
6. A polynomial of degree $n$ has **at most $n$ zeroes**.
7. For $x^2 + 7x + 10$: $\alpha + \beta = -7$, $\alpha\beta = 10$.
8. Example of a quadratic with no real zeroes: $x^2 + 1$.
9. If $x = 2$ is a zero of $x^2 - kx + 4$, then $k = 4$.
10. A cubic polynomial has at most **3** zeroes.

---

## Long Answer

1. $x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(x - 3)$; zeroes $2, 3$. Sum $= 5 = -(-5)/1$, Product $= 6 = 6/1$.
2. Quadratic polynomial with zeroes $-3, 4$: $x^2 - x - 12$.
3. For $2x^2 - 7x + 3$: $\alpha + \beta = \frac{7}{2}$, $\alpha\beta = \frac{3}{2}$, so $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = \frac{37}{4}$.
4. Verification of cubic zeroes: see [relationship-zeroes-coefficients.md](../handbook/concepts/relationship-zeroes-coefficients.md).
5. $3x^2 + x - 1 = (x + 1)(3x - 2) + 1$; quotient $3x - 2$, remainder $1$.
6. $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational (classic contradiction proof).
7. Given sum $-3$ and product $2$ for $ax^2 + bx + 6$: $a = 3$, $b = 9$.
8. Graph of $y = x^2 - 3x - 4$ is a parabola cutting the x-axis at $-1$ and $4$.

## Previous Year (CBSE)

1. Zeroes of $x^2 - 3x - 10$ are $5$ and $-2$; sum $= 3$, product $= -10$.
2. $\frac{1}{\alpha} + \frac{1}{\beta} = \frac{5}{3}$.
3. Quadratic polynomial with zeroes $2, -5$: $x^2 + 3x - 10$.
4. A degree-4 polynomial has **at most 4 zeroes**.
5. For $ax^2 - 6x - 6$, product $= \frac{-6}{a} = 4 \Rightarrow a = -\frac{3}{2}$.

## Challenge Problems

1. $\alpha^3 + \beta^3 = p^3 - 3pq$.
2. Zeroes of $x^4 - 13x^2 + 36$ are $\pm 2, \pm 3$.
3. For $3x^2 - 8x + k$ with reciprocal zeroes: $k = 3$.
4. For equal zeroes in $x^2 - 4x + k$: $k = 4$.
5. For $x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6$: $\alpha^2 + \beta^2 + \gamma^2 = 14$.
