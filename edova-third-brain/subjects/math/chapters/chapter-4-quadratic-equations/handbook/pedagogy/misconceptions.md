# Common Misconceptions

## 1. "$a$ must be positive"

Students sometimes believe $a$ cannot be negative. Clarify that $a \neq 0$ is the only requirement; it can be positive or negative.

## 2. Sign errors with $b$ and $c$

When using the quadratic formula, students often forget to include negative signs.

**Example:** For $x^2 - 5x - 6 = 0$, $b = -5$, not $5$.

## 3. Dividing only one term

When dividing by $a$ to complete the square, students may divide only the $x^2$ term.

**Correction:** Divide every term by $a$.

## 4. Forgetting $\pm$ in the formula

Students may write only $+$ or only $-$ and miss one root.

## 5. Roots vs factors confusion

Students may say "roots are $(x - 2)$ and $(x - 3)$" instead of "$x = 2$ and $x = 3$".

## 6. Rejecting valid negative roots

In word problems, students may reject negative roots automatically. Remind them to check the context.

## 7. Discriminant sign confusion

Students may think $D < 0$ means the equation has no solution at all, rather than no real solution.
