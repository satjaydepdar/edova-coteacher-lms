# Classroom Dialogue

## Opening Discussion

**Teacher:** "What is the highest power of $x$ in a linear equation?"

**Student:** "One."

**Teacher:** "What if the highest power is two? What might the equation look like?"

**Student:** "Something like $x^2 + ... = 0$."

**Teacher:** "That is a quadratic equation. Today we will learn how to solve them."

## On Factorisation

**Teacher:** "If $(x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$, what can we say?"

**Student:** "Either $x - 2 = 0$ or $x - 3 = 0$."

**Teacher:** "Why?"

**Student:** "Because if the product is zero, one of the factors must be zero."

## On the Discriminant

**Teacher:** "What does $b^2 - 4ac$ tell us?"

**Student:** "Whether the roots are real, equal, or not real."

**Teacher:** "Can we know this without actually finding the roots?"

**Student:** "Yes, just by calculating the discriminant."

## Closing Reflection

**Teacher:** "When would you prefer the quadratic formula over factorisation?"

**Student:** "When the equation is hard to factorise."
