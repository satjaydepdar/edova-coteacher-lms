# Solution by Factorisation

## What is a Root?

A real number $\alpha$ is called a **root** of the quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ if

$$a\alpha^2 + b\alpha + c = 0$$

We also say that $x = \alpha$ is a solution of the quadratic equation, or that $\alpha$ satisfies the equation.

The zeroes of the quadratic polynomial $ax^2 + bx + c$ and the roots of the quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ are the same.

A quadratic equation can have at most two roots.

## Principle

If $ab = 0$, then either $a = 0$ or $b = 0$.

If a quadratic expression can be written as a product of two linear factors equal to zero, we set each factor equal to zero and solve.

## Method

1. Factorise the quadratic expression $ax^2 + bx + c$ by splitting the middle term.
2. Write the equation as a product of two linear factors equal to zero.
3. Set each factor equal to zero.
4. Solve the two linear equations to get the roots.

## Example 3 from NCERT

Find the roots of $2x^2 - 5x + 3 = 0$ by factorisation.

Split $-5x$ as $-2x - 3x$ [because $(-2x) \times (-3x) = 6x^2 = (2x^2) \times 3$].

$$2x^2 - 5x + 3 = 2x^2 - 2x - 3x + 3$$

$$= 2x(x - 1) - 3(x - 1)$$

$$= (2x - 3)(x - 1)$$

So, $(2x - 3)(x - 1) = 0$

Therefore, $2x - 3 = 0$ or $x - 1 = 0$

$$x = \dfrac{3}{2} \text{ or } x = 1$$

## Example 4 from NCERT

Find the roots of $6x^2 - x - 2 = 0$.

Split $-x$ as $3x - 4x$.

$$6x^2 - x - 2 = 6x^2 + 3x - 4x - 2$$

$$= 3x(2x + 1) - 2(2x + 1)$$

$$= (3x - 2)(2x + 1)$$

So, $(3x - 2)(2x + 1) = 0$

Therefore, $3x - 2 = 0$ or $2x + 1 = 0$

$$x = \dfrac{2}{3} \text{ or } x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$$

## Example 5 from NCERT

Find the roots of $3x^2 - 2\sqrt{6}x + 2 = 0$.

Split $-2\sqrt{6}x$ as $-\sqrt{6}x - \sqrt{6}x$.

$$3x^2 - 2\sqrt{6}x + 2 = 3x^2 - \sqrt{6}x - \sqrt{6}x + 2$$

$$= \sqrt{3}x(\sqrt{3}x - \sqrt{2}) - \sqrt{2}(\sqrt{3}x - \sqrt{2})$$

$$= (\sqrt{3}x - \sqrt{2})(\sqrt{3}x - \sqrt{2})$$

So, $(\sqrt{3}x - \sqrt{2})^2 = 0$

Therefore, $\sqrt{3}x - \sqrt{2} = 0$

$$x = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}$$

This root is repeated twice.

## Example 6 from NCERT: Prayer Hall Dimensions

From Section 4.1, the breadth $x$ satisfies:

$$2x^2 + x - 300 = 0$$

Split $x$ as $-24x + 25x$:

$$2x^2 - 24x + 25x - 300 = 0$$

$$2x(x - 12) + 25(x - 12) = 0$$

$$(x - 12)(2x + 25) = 0$$

So, $x = 12$ or $x = -12.5$.

Since breadth cannot be negative, $x = 12$ m.

Length = $2x + 1 = 25$ m.
