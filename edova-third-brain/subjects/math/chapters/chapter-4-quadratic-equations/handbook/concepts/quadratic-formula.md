# Quadratic Formula

## The Formula

The roots of a quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, where $a \neq 0$, are given by

$$x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

provided $b^2 - 4ac \geq 0$.

## Historical Note

The textbook mentions that Sridharacharya (C.E. 1025) derived this formula, now known as the quadratic formula, for solving a quadratic equation by the method of completing the square.

## Using the Formula

1. Write the equation in standard form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$.
2. Identify $a$, $b$, and $c$ (with their signs).
3. Calculate $b^2 - 4ac$.
4. Substitute into the formula.
5. Simplify to find the roots.

## When to Use

Use the quadratic formula when factorisation is difficult or not obvious.

## Example from Nature of Roots

For $2x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0$:
- $a = 2$, $b = -4$, $c = 3$
- $b^2 - 4ac = 16 - 24 = -8 < 0$

Since the discriminant is negative, there are no real roots, so the formula does not give real values.
