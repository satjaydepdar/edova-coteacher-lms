# Chapter Summary: Quadratic Equations

## Key Points

1. A quadratic equation has the form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, where $a \neq 0$.
2. A real number $\alpha$ is a root of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ if $a\alpha^2 + b\alpha + c = 0$.
3. The zeroes of the quadratic polynomial $ax^2 + bx + c$ and the roots of the quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ are the same.
4. If we can factorise $ax^2 + bx + c$ into two linear factors, the roots are found by equating each factor to zero.
5. The roots of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ are given by the quadratic formula:

$$x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

6. A quadratic equation has:
   - two distinct real roots if $b^2 - 4ac > 0$
   - two equal roots if $b^2 - 4ac = 0$
   - no real roots if $b^2 - 4ac < 0$

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Forgetting that $a \neq 0$.
- Not simplifying the equation before deciding if it is quadratic.
- Sign errors when identifying $b$ and $c$ in the quadratic formula.
- Missing the $\pm$ sign in the quadratic formula.
- Not rejecting solutions that do not fit the context of the problem.
