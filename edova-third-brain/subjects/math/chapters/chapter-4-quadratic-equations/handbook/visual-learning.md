# Visual Learning: Quadratic Equations

## 1. Parabola Sketch

A quadratic equation $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ represents a **parabola**.

- If $a > 0$, the parabola opens **upwards**.
- If $a < 0$, the parabola opens **downwards**.
- The roots are the $x$-intercepts (where the graph crosses the x-axis).

### Nature of Roots from Graphs

| Discriminant | Graph Shape | Number of x-intercepts |
|---|---|---|
| $D > 0$ | Crosses x-axis at two points | 2 distinct real roots |
| $D = 0$ | Touches x-axis at one point | 1 repeated real root |
| $D < 0$ | Does not cross x-axis | No real roots |

## 2. Factorisation Area Model

For $x^2 + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)$, draw a rectangle with:
- Area $x^2$ (top-left)
- Areas $2x$ and $3x$ (top-right and bottom-left)
- Area $6$ (bottom-right)

## 3. Number Line for Roots

After finding roots, mark them on a number line to discuss intervals and verify solutions.

## 4. Prayer Hall Diagram

Draw a rectangle labelled:
- Breadth = $x$
- Length = $2x + 1$
- Area = 300 m²

This helps students visualise how $2x^2 + x - 300 = 0$ arises.

## Suggested Classroom Visual Aids

- Graph paper for sketching parabolas.
- GeoGebra / Desmos for dynamic exploration of roots and discriminant.
- Diagrams for word problems (park, pole, train, toys).
