# Case Study Example

## John and Jivanti's Marbles

John and Jivanti together have 45 marbles. Both of them lost 5 marbles each, and the product of the number of marbles they now have is 124.

### Information

- Total marbles = 45
- Each lost 5 marbles
- Product of remaining marbles = 124

### Questions

1. If John had $x$ marbles, how many did Jivanti have?
2. Form a quadratic equation for this situation.
3. Solve for $x$ and find how many marbles each had originally.

### Solutions

**1. Jivanti's marbles**

Jivanti had $(45 - x)$ marbles.

**2. Form the equation**

After losing 5 marbles each:
- John has $(x - 5)$ marbles.
- Jivanti has $(45 - x - 5) = (40 - x)$ marbles.

Product:

$$(x - 5)(40 - x) = 124$$

$$40x - x^2 - 200 + 5x = 124$$

$$-x^2 + 45x - 200 = 124$$

$$x^2 - 45x + 324 = 0$$

**3. Solve**

Factorise:

$$(x - 9)(x - 36) = 0$$

So $x = 9$ or $x = 36$.

If John had 9 marbles, Jivanti had 36 marbles.
If John had 36 marbles, Jivanti had 9 marbles.

Both are valid. The pair of numbers is 9 and 36.

### Reflection

This case study shows how a real-life situation can be translated into a quadratic equation. Note that both roots are valid here because the problem is symmetric in John and Jivanti.
