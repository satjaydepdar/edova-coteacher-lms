# Board Level Worked Examples

## Example 1: Nature of roots

Find the discriminant of $2x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0$ and hence find the nature of its roots.

**Solution:**

$a = 2$, $b = -4$, $c = 3$

$$b^2 - 4ac = (-4)^2 - 4(2)(3) = 16 - 24 = -8$$

Since $b^2 - 4ac < 0$, the equation has no real roots.

## Example 2: Quadratic formula

Find the roots of $3x^2 - 2\sqrt{6}x + 2 = 0$.

**Solution:**

$a = 3$, $b = -2\sqrt{6}$, $c = 2$

$$b^2 - 4ac = (-2\sqrt{6})^2 - 4(3)(2) = 24 - 24 = 0$$

Since the discriminant is 0, the roots are equal:

$$x = \dfrac{-b}{2a} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{6}}{6} = \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}$$

So both roots are $x = \dfrac{\sqrt{6}}{3}$.

## Example 3: Pole in circular park

A pole has to be erected at a point on the boundary of a circular park of diameter 13 metres in such a way that the differences of its distances from two diametrically opposite fixed gates A and B on the boundary is 7 metres. Is it possible to do so? If yes, at what distances from the two gates should the pole be erected?

**Solution:**

Let distance from gate B = $x$ m.
Then distance from gate A = $(x + 7)$ m.

Since AB is the diameter, $\angle APB = 90°$.

By Pythagoras theorem:

$$(x + 7)^2 + x^2 = 13^2$$
$$x^2 + 14x + 49 + x^2 = 169$$
$$2x^2 + 14x - 120 = 0$$
$$x^2 + 7x - 60 = 0$$

Discriminant:

$$b^2 - 4ac = 49 + 240 = 289 > 0$$

So, real roots exist. It is possible to erect the pole.

Using the quadratic formula:

$$x = \dfrac{-7 \pm 17}{2}$$

$x = 5$ or $x = -12$.

Since distance is positive, $x = 5$.

The pole should be 5 m from gate B and 12 m from gate A.

## Example 4: Rectangular park

Is it possible to design a rectangular park of perimeter 80 m and area 400 m²? If so, find its length and breadth.

**Solution:**

Let length = $l$, breadth = $b$.

$$2(l + b) = 80 \Rightarrow l + b = 40$$
$$lb = 400$$

Let $l = x$, then $b = 40 - x$.

$$x(40 - x) = 400$$
$$40x - x^2 = 400$$
$$x^2 - 40x + 400 = 0$$
$$(x - 20)^2 = 0$$

So $x = 20$.

Length = 20 m, Breadth = 20 m.

Yes, it is possible (it is a square).
