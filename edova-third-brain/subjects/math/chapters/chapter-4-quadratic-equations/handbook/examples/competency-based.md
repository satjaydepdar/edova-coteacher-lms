# Competency-Based Examples

## Example 1: Reasoning about the nature of roots

Show that the equation $x^2 + px - q^2 = 0$ has real roots for all real values of $p$ and $q$.

**Solution:**

$$b^2 - 4ac = p^2 - 4(1)(-q^2) = p^2 + 4q^2$$

Since $p^2 \geq 0$ and $4q^2 \geq 0$ for all real $p$, $q$:

$$p^2 + 4q^2 \geq 0$$

Hence, the equation always has real roots.

## Example 2: Error analysis

A student solved $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$ as follows:

$$(x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$$
$$x = 2 \text{ and } x = -3$$

Identify the error.

**Solution:**

The factorisation is correct, but the student made a sign error for the second factor.

From $x - 3 = 0$, we get $x = 3$, not $x = -3$.

Correct roots: $x = 2$ or $x = 3$.

## Example 3: Formulate and solve

The sum of the ages of two friends is 20 years. Four years ago, the product of their ages was 48. Is this possible? If so, determine their present ages.

**Solution:**

Let present ages be $x$ and $(20 - x)$ years.

Four years ago, their ages were $(x - 4)$ and $(16 - x)$.

$$(x - 4)(16 - x) = 48$$
$$16x - x^2 - 64 + 4x = 48$$
$$-x^2 + 20x - 64 = 48$$
$$x^2 - 20x + 112 = 0$$

Discriminant:

$$b^2 - 4ac = 400 - 448 = -48 < 0$$

Since the discriminant is negative, there are no real values of $x$.

Therefore, the situation is **not possible**.

## Example 4: Justify the method

When would you use the quadratic formula instead of factorisation to solve a quadratic equation?

**Solution:**

Use the quadratic formula when the quadratic expression cannot be easily factorised, such as when coefficients involve irrational numbers or when splitting the middle term is difficult. For example, $3x^2 - 2\sqrt{6}x + 2 = 0$ can be factorised, but $2x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0$ has no real roots, which is quickly seen from the discriminant.
