# Lesson 3

## Chapter 4: Quadratic Equations
### Topic: Nature of Roots (NCERT Section 4.4)

**Duration:** 45 minutes
**Target Audience:** Class X Students

---

### 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
- Use the discriminant to determine the nature of roots.
- Use the quadratic formula to find roots when they exist.
- Find unknown coefficients when the nature of roots is given.

---

### 2. Key Concepts

- Discriminant: $D = b^2 - 4ac$.
- Quadratic formula: $x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$.
- Nature of roots:
  - $D > 0$: two distinct real roots
  - $D = 0$: two equal real roots
  - $D < 0$: no real roots

---

### 3. Teaching-Learning Process

#### Introduction (10 mins)
- **Hook:** Can we tell if a quadratic equation has real roots without actually solving it?
- **Define:** Introduce discriminant and its role.

#### Main Activity (25 mins)
- **Example 7:** Discriminant of $2x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0$ — no real roots.
- **Example 8:** Pole in circular park — feasibility check using discriminant, then solve.
- **Example 9:** Discriminant of $3x^2 - 2x + \dfrac{1}{3} = 0$ — equal roots.
- **Find k for equal roots:** Exercise 4.3 question 2.
- **Student Practice:** Begin NCERT Exercise 4.3 in class.

#### Conclusion (10 mins)
- Summarise the three cases of the discriminant.
- Review the quadratic formula.
- Assign remaining questions from NCERT Exercise 4.3 as homework.

---

### 4. Assessment

- **Exit Ticket:**
  1. Find the discriminant of $x^2 - 6x + 9 = 0$ and state the nature of roots.
  2. Find $k$ if $kx(x - 2) + 6 = 0$ has equal roots.
