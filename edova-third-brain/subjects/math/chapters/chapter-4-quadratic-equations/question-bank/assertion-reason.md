# Assertion-Reason Questions

In each question, two statements are given:

- **Assertion (A)**
- **Reason (R)**

Choose the correct option:

(a) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A.
(b) Both A and R are true but R is NOT the correct explanation of A.
(c) A is true but R is false.
(d) A is false but R is true.

---

**Q1.** **Assertion (A):** The equation $x^2 + 4x + 4 = 0$ has equal roots.

**Reason (R):** A quadratic equation has equal roots if its discriminant is zero.

**Q2.** **Assertion (A):** The equation $2x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0$ has no real roots.

**Reason (R):** If the discriminant $b^2 - 4ac < 0$, the quadratic equation has no real roots.

**Q3.** **Assertion (A):** The quadratic formula for $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ is $x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$.

**Reason (R):** The roots of a quadratic equation can be found by equating each factor to zero.

**Q4.** **Assertion (A):** The equation $x^2 - 3x - 10 = 0$ can be solved by factorisation.

**Reason (R):** Every quadratic equation has real roots.

**Q5.** **Assertion (A):** The zeroes of the quadratic polynomial $ax^2 + bx + c$ and the roots of the quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ are the same.

**Reason (R):** A real number $\alpha$ is a root of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ if $a\alpha^2 + b\alpha + c = 0$.

---

## Answers

1. (a) Both A and R are true and R correctly explains A.
2. (a) Both A and R are true and R correctly explains A.
3. (b) Both A and R are true, but R does not explain A (R describes factorisation, not the formula).
4. (c) A is true but R is false (not every quadratic equation has real roots).
5. (a) Both A and R are true and R correctly explains A.
