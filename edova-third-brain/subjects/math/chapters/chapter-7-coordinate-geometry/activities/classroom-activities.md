# Classroom Activities

Activities to be conducted in the classroom for Chapter 7: Coordinate Geometry.

---

## Activity 1: Coordinate Picture Plotting (Graph Art)

**Time:** 20 minutes | **Group Size:** Individual | **Materials:** Graph paper, ruler, pencil

### Instructions
1. Draw a pair of perpendicular axes on graph paper. Choose a suitable scale (1 unit = 1 cm).
2. Plot the following points and join them in order:
   - A(4, 8) → B(3, 9) → C(3, 8) → D(1, 6) → E(1, 5) → F(3, 3) → G(6, 3) → H(8, 5) → I(8, 6) → J(6, 8) → K(6, 9) → L(5, 8) → A
3. Form triangles by joining:
   - P(3.5, 7), Q(3, 6), R(4, 6)
   - X(5.5, 7), Y(5, 6), Z(6, 6)
   - S(4, 5), T(4.5, 4), U(5, 5)
4. Join S to (0, 5) and (0, 6). Join U to (9, 5) and (9, 6).
5. **Question:** What picture have you got? Describe it in words.

### Learning Outcome
Reinforces accurate plotting of points with fractional coordinates and understanding that ordered pairs uniquely determine positions on a plane.

## Activity 2: Discovering the Distance Formula

**Time:** 25 minutes | **Group Size:** Pairs | **Materials:** Graph paper, ruler

### Instructions
1. Each pair plots two general points P(x₁, y₁) and Q(x₂, y₂) in the first quadrant on graph paper.
2. Draw perpendiculars PR and QS to the x-axis from P and Q respectively.
3. Draw PT perpendicular to QS, meeting QS at T.
4. Measure and label:
   - PT = |x₂ − x₁|
   - QT = |y₂ − y₁|
5. Apply Pythagoras Theorem to right triangle PTQ:
   - PQ² = PT² + QT²
   - PQ² = (x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²
   - PQ = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]
6. Verify with a numerical example: find the distance between (4, 6) and (6, 8) both by measurement and by formula.

### Learning Outcome
Students derive the distance formula from Pythagoras Theorem rather than memorising it, building conceptual understanding.

## Activity 3: Collinearity Check — Group Challenge

**Time:** 15 minutes | **Group Size:** Groups of 4 | **Materials:** Worksheet

### Instructions
Each group receives three sets of points. For each set, determine if the points are collinear using the distance formula:

- **Set A:** (1, 5), (2, 3), (−2, −11)
- **Set B:** (3, 1), (5, 3), (9, 7)
- **Set C:** (2, 4), (5, 7), (8, 10)
- **Set D:** (1, 2), (4, 5), (7, 9)

### Method
For points A, B, C — calculate AB, BC, and AC. If AB + BC = AC (or any similar combination), the points are collinear.

### Group Task
- Identify which sets are collinear and which are not.
- For non-collinear sets, state what type of triangle is formed (using the converse of Pythagoras Theorem).
- The first group to correctly classify all four sets wins.

### Learning Outcome
Students practise applying the distance formula repeatedly and understand the additive property of distances on a straight line.

## Activity 4: Quadrilateral Identification Challenge

**Time:** 20 minutes | **Group Size:** Pairs | **Materials:** Calculator, worksheet

### Instructions
You are given the vertices of quadrilaterals. Use the distance formula to find all four sides and both diagonals, then identify the type of quadrilateral:

1. **(−1, −2), (1, 0), (−1, 2), (−3, 0)**
2. **(1, 7), (4, 2), (−1, −1), (−4, 4)**
3. **(4, 5), (7, 6), (4, 3), (1, 2)**
4. **(−3, 5), (3, 1), (0, 3), (−1, −4)**

### Decision Criteria
| Property | Square | Rectangle | Rhombus | Parallelogram |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All sides equal | Yes | No | Yes | No |
| Diagonals equal | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| One angle = 90° | Yes | Yes | No | No |
| Opposite sides equal | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |

### Learning Outcome
Students learn to systematically verify geometric properties using algebraic tools — a core skill in coordinate geometry.

## Activity 5: Section Formula Relay Race

**Time:** 20 minutes | **Group Size:** Teams of 3 | **Materials:** Worksheet

### Rules
Each team member solves one part and passes the answer to the next member who needs it.

**Task Chain (for each team):**

1. **Member A:** Find the coordinates of the point that divides the line segment joining (4, −3) and (8, 5) in the ratio 3 : 1.
2. **Member B:** Using Member A's answer as one vertex, and given (2, −2) and (−7, 4), find the points of trisection of this segment.
3. **Member C:** Using the mid-point from Member B's answer and the point (6, 1), find the fourth vertex of a parallelogram if (8, 2) and (9, 4) are the other two vertices.

### Scoring
- Correct answer for each part: 10 points
- First team to finish: +5 bonus points
- All three correct in sequence: +10 bonus points

### Learning Outcome
Builds speed and accuracy with the section formula and mid-point formula while demonstrating how these concepts chain together.

## Activity 6: Equidistant Point — Locus Exploration

**Time:** 15 minutes | **Group Size:** Individual | **Materials:** Graph paper

### Instructions
1. Plot points A(7, 1) and B(3, 5) on graph paper.
2. Find 5–6 points that are equidistant from A and B by either:
   - Using the condition AP² = BP² and choosing x-values to solve for y, or
   - Constructing the perpendicular bisector with compass and ruler.
3. Plot all equidistant points you found and join them.
4. Verify algebraically: show that the equation of this line is x − y = 2.
5. **Extension:** Repeat for A(6, 5) and B(−4, 3). Find the point on the y-axis equidistant from both.

### Learning Outcome
Students discover that the set of all points equidistant from two fixed points forms the perpendicular bisector — a foundational concept for loci.

## Activity 7: Classroom Seating Collinearity Check

**Time:** 10 minutes | **Group Size:** Pairs | **Materials:** None (use actual classroom)

### Instructions
1. Choose any three seated classmates. Assign coordinates based on their row and column position (e.g., 3rd row, 1st column = (3, 1)).
2. Apply the distance formula to check if the three students are seated in a straight line.
3. Try to find at least one collinear triplet and one non-collinear triplet.
4. For the non-collinear triplet, identify the type of triangle formed.

### Learning Outcome
Connects abstract coordinate geometry to the immediate physical environment, making the concept of collinearity tangible.

## Quick-Fire Round (5 minutes)

The teacher calls out questions; students raise hands to answer:

1. What is the distance of (5, 12) from the origin?
2. If a point divides a segment in the ratio 1:1, what formula do you use?
3. What is the distance between two points on the x-axis with coordinates (a, 0) and (b, 0)?
4. State the condition for three points to be collinear using distances.
5. If all sides of a quadrilateral are equal and one angle is 90°, what is it?
6. What is the distance between (0, 3) and (0, 8)?
7. The midpoint of (2, −2) and (−7, 4) is?
8. How many points of trisection does a line segment have?
