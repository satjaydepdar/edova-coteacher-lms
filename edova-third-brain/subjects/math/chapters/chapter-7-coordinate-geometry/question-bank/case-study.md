## Case Study 1 — Airport Runway System

An airport has three runways represented by line segments on a coordinate grid (1 unit = 100 m):
- Runway A: from R₁(2, 1) to R₂(8, 7)
- Runway B: from R₃(2, 7) to R₄(8, 1)
- Runway C: from R₅(5, 0) to R₆(5, 8)

**Q1 (1 mark):** Find the length of Runway A.
**Q2 (1 mark):** Find the midpoint of Runway C.
**Q3 (1 mark):** The airport authority claims Runways A and B are of equal length. Verify.
**Q4 (1 mark):** A control tower is to be placed at the intersection of Runways A and B. If they intersect at their midpoints, find the tower's coordinates.

## Case Study 2 — Agricultural Field

A farmer's triangular field has vertices at P(0, 0), Q(10, 0) and R(4, 8) on a survey map (1 unit = 10 m).

**Q1 (1 mark):** Find the length of the side PQ in metres.
**Q2 (1 mark):** A well is to be dug at the centroid of the field. Find the coordinates of the centroid using the section formula (by finding the point that divides the median in ratio 2:1).
**Q3 (2 marks):** The farmer wants to fence the field. Find the total length of fencing needed (perimeter) in metres.
**Q4 (1 mark):** Is the field a right-angled triangle? Justify.

## Answers

CS1: A1: √[36+36] = 6√2 units = 600√2 m. A2: (5, 4). A3: RB = √[36+36] = 6√2. Yes, equal. A4: Midpoint of both = (5, 4).

CS2: A1: 10 units = 100 m. A2: Median from R: midpoint of PQ = (5, 0). Centroid divides median 2:1 from R: ((2×5+1×4)/3, (2×0+1×8)/3) = (14/3, 8/3). A3: PQ=100m, QR=√[36+64]×10=√1000×10=100√10 m, PR=√[16+64]×10=√80×10=100√8 m. Total = 100(1+√10+√8) m. A4: PQ²=100, PR²=800, QR²=1000. PR²+PQ²=900≠1000=QR². No, not right-angled.
