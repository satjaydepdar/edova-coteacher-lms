## Questions

**Q1.** A farmer has a triangular field with vertices at A(0, 0), B(4, 0) and C(0, 3). He wants to install a water pump at a point equidistant from all three vertices. Does such a point exist? If yes, find its coordinates.

**Q2.** Three schools A, B, C are located at (1, 4), (5, 1) and (9, 4) respectively on a town map. A bus depot must be placed at the midpoint of the route from A to C. Find the depot's coordinates and the distance from the depot to school B.

**Q3.** A drone flies from point P(2, 3) to Q(8, 7). At what point does a second drone, which starts at P and flies at double the speed of the first drone, meet the first drone if both start simultaneously and the second drone flies toward Q?

**Q4.** Given four points, a student calculates all four sides and gets equal values. The student concludes it is a square. Is this conclusion always correct? Justify with an example.

**Q5.** The coordinates of the midpoint of a line segment are (2, 3). If one endpoint is (6, 5), find the other endpoint. If this segment is a diagonal of a square with sides parallel to the axes, find all four vertices.

## Answers

A1. The point equidistant from all three vertices is the circumcentre. For a right triangle (which this is, since AB² + AC² = BC²), the circumcentre is the midpoint of the hypotenuse. Hypotenuse BC: midpoint = (2, 1.5).

A2. Midpoint of AC = (5, 4). Distance to B(5, 1) = 3 units.

A3. The second drone covers twice the distance in the same time, so it meets the first drone at the point that divides PQ in ratio 2:1 (from P). Using section formula: P = (2×8+1×2)/3, (2×7+1×3)/3 = (6, 17/3).

A4. No. A rhombus also has all four sides equal. Example: (0, 0), (1, 2), (3, 2), (2, 0) — all sides √5 but diagonals √13 and √5 (not equal), so it's a rhombus, not a square.

A5. Other endpoint: M = ((6+x)/2, (5+y)/2) = (2, 3) → x = −2, y = 1. Other endpoint = (−2, 1). Square vertices: (−2, 1), (6, 1), (6, 5), (−2, 5).
