## Statement

The midpoint M of the line segment joining P(x₁, y₁) and Q(x₂, y₂) is:

**M = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)**

## Derivation

This is the section formula with m₁ : m₂ = 1 : 1.

x = (1·x₂ + 1·x₁)/(1 + 1) = (x₁ + x₂)/2
y = (1·y₂ + 1·y₁)/(1 + 1) = (y₁ + y₂)/2

**Teaching note:** Do NOT teach this as a separate formula to memorise. Show it as a special case of section formula.

## Applications in this chapter

### Parallelogram diagonals
If ABCD is a parallelogram, the midpoints of AC and BD coincide.
Midpoint of AC = Midpoint of BD → two equations to solve for unknowns.

### Circle with given centre and endpoint of diameter
If AB is a diameter and centre is known, the centre is the midpoint of AB.

### Rhombus area via diagonals
Area of rhombus = ½ × d₁ × d₂, where d₁ and d₂ are diagonal lengths found using the distance formula.

### Dividing a segment into equal parts
To find points dividing AB into 4 equal parts, use section formula with ratios 1:3, 1:1, 3:1.

## Common student errors

| Error | Fix |
|-------|-----|
| Adding x of one point with y of the other | Keep x and y separate |
| Forgetting to divide by 2 | Write the formula in full before substituting |
| Not setting up equations for parallelogram | Write "Midpoint of AC = Midpoint of BD" then equate |
