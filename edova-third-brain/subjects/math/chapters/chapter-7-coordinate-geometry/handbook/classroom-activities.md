## Activity 1: Coordinate Aeroplane (10 minutes)
Use the textbook's opening activity. Give students the list of 12 points. They plot and connect to discover an aeroplane. Then ask: "What is the distance from the nose to the tail?" — applies the formula to their own creation.

## Activity 2: Human Coordinate System (15 minutes)
Create a large grid on the floor with tape. Students stand at coordinates. Call out:
- "Everyone on the x-axis, wave!" (checks understanding)
- "Person at (3, 4), walk to (7, 1). Class, how far did they walk?"
- "Two people at (1, 2) and (5, 6). Third person, stand at their midpoint."

## Activity 3: Formula Relay Race (10 minutes)
Divide class into teams of 4. Each team gets 4 problems:
- Student 1: Find the distance (pass answer to Student 2)
- Student 2: Find the midpoint of the same segment (pass to Student 3)
- Student 3: Find the point that divides the segment in ratio 2:1 (pass to Student 4)
- Student 4: Verify all answers and write them on the board
First team with all correct answers wins.

## Activity 4: Mystery Figure (15 minutes)
Give each pair of students a set of 4 coordinates (different for each pair). They must:
1. Plot the points
2. Calculate all sides and diagonals
3. Identify the figure type
4. Create a "mystery figure" for another pair (must be a valid geometric figure)

## Activity 5: Real-World Mapping (20 minutes)
Provide a simple map of the school or locality with a coordinate grid overlay. Students:
1. Identify coordinates of key landmarks
2. Find distances between landmarks
3. Find the midpoint of a pathway
4. Determine if three landmarks are collinear

## Activity 6: Error Correction Gallery Walk (15 minutes)
Post 6 "solutions" around the room, each with exactly one error. Students walk around, find the errors, and write corrections. Debrief as a class.
