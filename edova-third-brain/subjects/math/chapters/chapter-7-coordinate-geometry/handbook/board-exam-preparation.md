## Exam pattern analysis

Coordinate Geometry typically appears as:
- One 2-mark question (short answer)
- One 3-mark question (short answer)
- One 4-mark question (long answer)
- Sometimes part of a competency-based question

Total: typically 7–9 marks from this chapter.

## Frequently tested question types

### Most frequent (appears almost every year)
1. Prove that given points form a specific quadrilateral (4 marks)
2. Find the ratio in which a point divides a segment (3 marks)
3. Find a point on an axis equidistant from two points (2–3 marks)

### Frequent
4. Find the coordinates of trisection points (3 marks)
5. Find the relation between x and y for equidistant condition (3 marks)
6. Find unknown coordinate in a parallelogram (3–4 marks)

### Occasional
7. Find the area of a rhombus (3–4 marks)
8. Case study involving Sports Day or classroom seating (4–5 marks)
9. Assertion-Reason question (1 mark)

## Exam day tips

1. **Always write the formula** before substituting — earns partial marks even if calculation is wrong
2. **Draw a diagram** for figure verification problems — examiners look for it
3. **Verify your answer** — at least substitute back once
4. **For quadrilateral problems**, calculate ALL four sides AND BOTH diagonals — don't skip
5. **For ratio problems**, always verify with the other coordinate
6. **For equidistant problems**, expand carefully — sign errors are the #1 mistake
7. **Label your final answer** clearly — "Therefore, ABCD is a square."

## Time management

- 2-mark question: 3–4 minutes
- 3-mark question: 5–6 minutes
- 4-mark question: 7–8 minutes
- If stuck, move on and return later
