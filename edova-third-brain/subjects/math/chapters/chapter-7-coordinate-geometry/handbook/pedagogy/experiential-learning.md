## Activity 1: Classroom Coordinate System

Tape a large coordinate grid on the classroom floor. Students physically stand at coordinates. Call out problems:

- "Student at (3, 2), walk to (7, 5). How many units did you travel?" (Use distance formula to verify)
- "Student at (1, 4) and student at (5, 4), where should a third student stand to be the midpoint?"
- "Three students stand at points. Class, are they collinear? How can we check?"

## Activity 2: Outdoor Distance Measurement

Mark two points on the school ground representing "Town A" and "Town B" with known east-west and north-south displacements. Students measure actual distance with a measuring tape and verify using the distance formula.

**Extension:** Place a third point and check collinearity.

## Activity 3: Relay Tower Simulation

Recreate the textbook's relay tower problem physically. Two students stand as "Town A" (origin) and "Town B" (36 east, 15 north). A third student must position themselves so their distance from B is twice their distance from A. The class calculates where they should stand using the section formula, then verifies.

## Activity 4: Map Reading

Provide a simple map with a coordinate grid overlay. Students:
- Find distances between landmarks
- Identify the midpoint of a road segment
- Determine if three landmarks are collinear
- Find a point that divides a road segment in a given ratio

## Reflection

After each activity, ask: "Where did you use the formula in real life? How did the physical experience help you understand the algebra?"
