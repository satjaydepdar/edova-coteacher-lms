## Section 7.2 — Distance Formula

### Opening hook (5 minutes)
Use the textbook's coordinate picture activity (joining points A through L). Students plot points and discover it forms an aeroplane. This activates prior knowledge before any formula.

### Derivation strategy
Do NOT start with the general formula. Follow the textbook's build-up:
1. Same axis → simple subtraction
2. Different axes → Pythagoras directly
3. Same quadrant → construct right triangle
4. Different quadrants → same method, bigger numbers
5. General case → abstract derivation

### When to introduce the formula
After case 4. Let students do cases 1–3 without any formula. By case 4, they'll want a shortcut. THAT is when you introduce the formula.

## Section 7.3 — Section Formula

### Opening hook
The relay tower problem: "A phone company needs to place a tower between two towns. The tower must be twice as far from Town B as from Town A. Where should it go?"

### The m₁/m₂ confusion
Strategies:
1. Colour code on the board: m₁ in red with x₂ in red
2. Verbal pattern: "First multiplier, second point. Second multiplier, first point."
3. Always verify by checking if the point is in the right direction
4. Practice ratio identification separately before combining with the formula

### Midpoint as special case
Derive midpoint ONCE from section formula. Never teach it as isolated.

## Common teaching mistakes to avoid

| Mistake | Alternative |
|---------|-------------|
| Giving the formula first | Build up from cases, introduce formula as shortcut |
| Not verifying answers | Always substitute back, check on diagram |
| Skipping the derivation | Always derive, even briefly |
| Doing all examples yourself | Do 1-2, then students do the rest |
| Not connecting to geometry | Constantly ask: "What shape? What property?" |
