## Misconception 1: "Distance formula is a new formula"

**What students think:** "This is a completely new thing I need to memorise."
**Reality:** It is literally Pythagoras' theorem applied to a right triangle constructed from coordinates.
**Correction strategy:** Always derive it. Never present it as a given. Use the "same axis → different axes → general" build-up.

## Misconception 2: "m₁ always goes with x₁"

**What students think:** Subscript matching — m₁ with x₁, m₂ with x₂.
**Reality:** m₁ goes with x₂ (the second point). The subscripts are deliberately crossed.
**Correction strategy:** Use the "first multiplier, second point" chant. Colour-code on the board. Verify every answer by checking direction.

## Misconception 3: "If three points have equal distances, they form an equilateral triangle"

**What students think:** AB = BC = CA → equilateral. This part is correct.
**Where it goes wrong:** They assume equal distances without calculating, or they miscalculate one distance and conclude incorrectly.
**Correction strategy:** Always compute all three distances explicitly. Write them out before making any claim.

## Misconception 4: "Collinear means the middle point's distance from each endpoint must be equal"

**What students think:** AB = BC means collinear.
**Reality:** AB = BC with a specific angle could be an isosceles triangle. Collinearity requires AB + BC = AC.
**Correction strategy:** Teach the specific condition: "Sum of two smaller distances = largest distance." Practice identifying the largest distance first.

## Misconception 5: "AP = 3/7 AB means the ratio is 3:7"

**What students think:** The fraction directly gives the ratio.
**Reality:** If AP = 3/7 of AB, then PB = 4/7 of AB, so AP:PB = 3:4.
**Correction strategy:** Draw the segment, mark the fraction visually, then convert to ratio. Practice with several examples.

## Misconception 6: "For a square, checking all sides equal is enough"

**What students think:** 4 equal sides → square.
**Reality:** A rhombus also has 4 equal sides. A square needs equal sides AND equal diagonals.
**Correction strategy:** Teach the decision tree. "Equal sides alone → rhombus. Equal sides + equal diagonals → square."

## Misconception 7: "The perpendicular bisector is just a formula result"

**What students think:** x − y = 2 is just an equation to write and move on.
**Reality:** It represents ALL points equidistant from A and B. It's a geometric object.
**Correction strategy:** After finding the relation, plot it. Show that the line passes through the midpoint and is perpendicular to AB.
