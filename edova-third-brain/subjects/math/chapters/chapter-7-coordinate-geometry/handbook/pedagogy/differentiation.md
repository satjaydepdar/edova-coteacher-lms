## For struggling learners (Tier 1)

- Provide pre-drawn coordinate grids for every problem
- Start with integer coordinates only (no fractions, no surds in answers)
- Use the step-by-step build-up (same axis → different axes → general) with extra practice at each stage
- Provide a formula card with all three formulas and the m₁/x₂ rule highlighted
- Pair with a stronger student for derivation activities
- Reduce the number of problems: 3 well-done problems > 8 rushed ones

## For on-grade learners (Tier 2)

- Standard NCERT problems and examples
- Mix of integer and simple surd answers
- Expected to derive formulas with guidance
- Should verify their own answers
- 5–7 problems per practice set

## For advanced learners (Tier 3)

- Problems where the ratio or coordinates must be determined (not just substituted)
- Multi-step: find a point, then verify a property, then find another point
- Challenge: "Given three vertices of a parallelogram, find the fourth" (two possible answers)
- Extension: derive the condition for four points to be concyclic (beyond syllabus but engaging)
- Ask them to create their own problems and exchange with peers

## Example of a tiered problem

**Tier 1:** Find the distance between (1, 2) and (4, 6).

**Tier 2:** Find the distance between (−3, 4) and (2, −1). Express the answer in simplest surd form.

**Tier 3:** The distance between (a, 3) and (5, a) is √41. Find the possible values of a.

## CoTeacher prompt

```
Take this coordinate geometry problem and create three
differentiated versions:
"Find the distance between the points A(2, 3) and B(6, 7)."

Tier 1 (Approaching): Same problem with a grid provided,
step-by-step blanks to fill in.
Tier 2 (Meeting): Standard problem as stated.
Tier 3 (Exceeding): "If A(2, 3) and B(6, 7) are two vertices
of a square, find the other two vertices."
```
