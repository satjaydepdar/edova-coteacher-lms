## What this chapter covers

Coordinate geometry is the algebraic study of geometry — using coordinates and formulas to solve problems about distances, midpoints, and division of line segments. It bridges what students know about algebra (equations, substitution) with what they know about geometry (shapes, properties).

## Three main topics

| Topic | Section | Core Formula | Key Application |
|-------|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| Distance Formula | 7.2 | PQ = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²] | Collinearity, figure verification, equidistant points |
| Section Formula | 7.3 | P = (m₁x₂+m₂x₁)/(m₁+m₂), (m₁y₂+m₂y₁)/(m₁+m₂) | Finding division points, trisection, parallelogram diagonals |
| Midpoint Formula | 7.3 (special case) | M = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2) | Centre of circles, parallelogram diagonals, rhombus area |

## What is NOT in this chapter (common confusion)

- **Area of triangle formula** — NOT in Class X NCERT
- **External division** — mentioned in "Note to Reader" but not examined
- **Equation of a line** — not in this chapter
- **Slope concept** — not formally introduced here

## Suggested time allocation (8 periods)

| Period | Topic | Activity |
|--------|-------|----------|
| 1 | Introduction, recap, distance on axes | Plotting activity, Pythagoras recap |
| 2 | Distance formula — derivation and basic problems | Guided derivation, worked examples |
| 3 | Distance formula — applications (collinearity, figure types) | Group problem-solving |
| 4 | Section formula — derivation | Guided derivation with relay tower example |
| 5 | Section formula — problems (trisection, finding ratios) | Mixed practice |
| 6 | Midpoint formula, parallelogram properties | Discovery activity |
| 7 | Mixed practice and word problems | Worksheet + discussion |
| 8 | Revision and assessment | Quiz or board-style practice |

## Dependencies within the chapter

Distance formula is independent. Section formula builds on the idea of ratios but does NOT depend on the distance formula. Midpoint formula is a special case of section formula. All three can be taught somewhat independently but are best done in sequence for coherence.
