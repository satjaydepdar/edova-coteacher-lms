## Example 1 — Checking collinearity

Determine if the points (1, 5), (2, 3) and (−2, −11) are collinear.

**Solution:**
AB = √[(2−1)² + (3−5)²] = √[1 + 4] = √5
BC = √[(−2−2)² + (−11−3)²] = √[16 + 196] = √212
AC = √[(−2−1)² + (−11−5)²] = √[9 + 256] = √265

Check: AB + BC = √5 + √212 ≠ √265 = AC
Therefore, the points are NOT collinear.

## Example 2 — Finding the ratio (given dividing point)

In what ratio does the point (−4, 6) divide the segment joining (−6, 10) and (3, −8)?

**Solution:**
Let the ratio be k : 1.
x = (k×3 + 1×(−6))/(k + 1) = −4
3k − 6 = −4k − 4
7k = 2
k = 2/7
Ratio = 2 : 7

Verify with y:
y = (2/7 × (−8) + 10)/(2/7 + 1) = (−16/7 + 70/7)/(9/7) = 54/9 = 6 ✓

## Example 3 — Isosceles triangle verification

Check whether (5, −2), (6, 4) and (7, −2) are the vertices of an isosceles triangle.

**Solution:**
AB = √[(6−5)² + (4+2)²] = √[1 + 36] = √37
BC = √[(7−6)² + (−2−4)²] = √[1 + 36] = √37
AC = √[(7−5)² + (−2+2)²] = √[4 + 0] = 2

Since AB = BC = √37, two sides are equal.
Also AB + BC > AC, so a triangle is formed.
It is an isosceles triangle.

## Example 4 — Trisection of a segment

Find the points of trisection of the segment joining (2, −2) and (−7, 4).

**Solution:**
Let P and Q be the trisection points.
P divides AB in ratio 1:2:
P = (1×(−7) + 2×2)/(1+2), (1×4 + 2×(−2))/(1+2))
  = (−7 + 4)/3, (4 − 4)/3)
  = (−1, 0)

Q divides AB in ratio 2:1:
Q = (2×(−7) + 1×2)/(2+1), (2×4 + 1×(−2))/(2+1))
  = (−14 + 2)/3, (8 − 2)/3)
  = (−4, 2)

Trisection points: (−1, 0) and (−4, 2).

## Example 5 — Parallelogram vertex

If (1, 2), (4, y), (x, 6) and (3, 5) are vertices of a parallelogram in order, find x and y.

**Solution:**
Diagonals bisect each other: midpoint of AC = midpoint of BD.
Midpoint of AC = ((1+x)/2, (2+6)/2) = ((1+x)/2, 4)
Midpoint of BD = ((4+3)/2, (y+5)/2) = (7/2, (y+5)/2)

Equating: (1+x)/2 = 7/2 → x = 6
4 = (y+5)/2 → y = 3
