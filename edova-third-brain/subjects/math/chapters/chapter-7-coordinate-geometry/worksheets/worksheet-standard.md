### Collinearity & Triangle Verification

**Q1.** Determine if the points $(1, 5)$, $(2, 3)$ and $(-2, -11)$ are collinear.

**Q2.** Check whether $(5, -2)$, $(6, 4)$ and $(7, -2)$ are the vertices of an isosceles triangle.

### Quadrilateral Verification

**Q3.** Name the type of quadrilateral formed, if any, by the following points, and give reasons:

(i) $(-1, -2)$, $(1, 0)$, $(-1, 2)$, $(-3, 0)$ 
(ii) $(-3, 5)$, $(3, 1)$, $(0, 3)$, $(-1, -4)$ 
(iii) $(4, 5)$, $(7, 6)$, $(4, 3)$, $(1, 2)$ 
### Points on Axes & Equidistant Points

**Q4.** Find the point on the x-axis which is equidistant from $(2, -5)$ and $(-2, 9)$.

**Q5.** Find the values of $y$ for which the distance between the points $P(2, -3)$ and $Q(10, y)$ is $10$ units.

**Q6.** Find a relation between $x$ and $y$ such that the point $(x, y)$ is equidistant from the point $(3, 6)$ and $(-3, 4)$.

### Section Formula (Internal Division & Trisection)

**Q7.** Find the coordinates of the points of trisection of the line segment joining $(4, -1)$ and $(-2, -3)$.

**Q8.** Find the ratio in which the line segment joining the points $(-3, 10)$ and $(6, -8)$ is divided by $(-1, 6)$.

**Q9.** Find the ratio in which the line segment joining $A(1, -5)$ and $B(-4, 5)$ is divided by the x-axis. Also find the coordinates of the point of division.

**Q10.** If $(1, 2)$, $(4, y)$, $(x, 6)$ and $(3, 5)$ are the vertices of a parallelogram taken in order, find $x$ and $y$.

**Q11.** If A and B are $(-2, -2)$ and $(2, -4)$ respectively, find the coordinates of P such that $AP = \dfrac{3}{7}AB$ and P lies on the line segment AB.

**Q12.** Find the area of a rhombus if its vertices are $(3, 0)$, $(4, 5)$, $(-1, 4)$ and $(-2, -1)$ taken in order. \hfill *[Hint: Area of a rhombus = $\frac{1}{2}$ (product of its diagonals)]*

**Q1.** $AB = \sqrt{1+4}=\sqrt{5}$, $BC = \sqrt{16+196}=\sqrt{212}$, $AC = \sqrt{9+256}=\sqrt{265}$ 
Since $AB + BC \neq AC$ (numerically $\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{212} \neq \sqrt{265}$), the points are **not collinear**.

**Q2.** $AB = \sqrt{1+36}=\sqrt{37}$, $BC = \sqrt{1+36}=\sqrt{37}$, $AC = \sqrt{4+0}=2$ 
 $AB = BC$, so the triangle is **isosceles**.

**Q3.** (i) $AB = BC = CD = DA = 2\sqrt{2}$; $AC = BD = 4$. All sides equal, both diagonals equal → **Square**.

(ii) $AB = \sqrt{52}$, $BC = \sqrt{13}$, $CD = \sqrt{50}$, $DA = \sqrt{85}$. All sides different → **No specific quadrilateral** (a general quadrilateral).

(iii) $AB = CD = \sqrt{10}$, $BC = DA = 3\sqrt{2}$. Opposite sides equal. Diagonals: $AC = 2$, $BD = \sqrt{52}$. Diagonals not equal → **Parallelogram** (not a rectangle).

**Q4.** Let point be $(x, 0)$: $(x-2)^2 + 25 = (x+2)^2 + 81$ 
 $\Rightarrow -8x = 56 \Rightarrow x = -7$. Point is $(-7,\; 0)$.

**Q5.** $\sqrt{64 + (y+3)^2} = 10 \Rightarrow (y+3)^2 = 36 \Rightarrow y+3 = \pm 6$ 
 $y = 3$ or $y = -9$.

**Q6.** $(x-3)^2+(y-6)^2 = (x+3)^2+(y-4)^2$ 
 $\Rightarrow -6x-12y+45 = 6x-8y+25 \Rightarrow 3x+y = 5$.

**Q7.** Let $A(4,-1)$, $B(-2,-3)$.

 $P$ (divides in $1:2$) $= \left(\dfrac{1(-2)+2(4)}{3},\; \dfrac{1(-3)+2(-1)}{3}\right) = \left(2,\; -\dfrac{5}{3}\right)$ 
 $Q$ (divides in $2:1$) $= \left(\dfrac{2(-2)+1(4)}{3},\; \dfrac{2(-3)+1(-1)}{3}\right) = \left(0,\; -\dfrac{7}{3}\right)$ 
**Q8.** Let ratio be $k:1$: $-1 = \dfrac{6k-3}{k+1} \Rightarrow -k-1=6k-3 \Rightarrow k = \dfrac{2}{7}$ 
Ratio is $\mathbf{2:7}$. (Verified with y-coordinate: $6 = \dfrac{-8k+10}{k+1}$ also gives $k=\frac{2}{7}$.)

**Q9.** On x-axis, $y=0$. Let ratio be $k:1$: $0 = \dfrac{5k-5}{k+1} \Rightarrow k=1$ 
Ratio is $\mathbf{1:1}$. Point $= \left(\dfrac{1(-4)+1(1)}{2},\; 0\right) = \left(-\dfrac{3}{2},\; 0\right)$.

**Q10.** Midpoint of AC = Midpoint of BD:

 $\dfrac{1+x}{2} = \dfrac{4+3}{2} \Rightarrow x = 6$; \quad $\dfrac{2+6}{2} = \dfrac{y+5}{2} \Rightarrow y = 3$.

**Q11.** $AP = \frac{3}{7}AB$ means P divides AB in ratio $3:4$.

 $P = \left(\dfrac{3(2)+4(-2)}{7},\; \dfrac{3(-4)+4(-2)}{7}\right) = \left(-\dfrac{2}{7},\; -\dfrac{20}{7}\right)$.

**Q12.** Diagonal $AC = \sqrt{(-1-3)^2+(4-0)^2} = \sqrt{32} = 4\sqrt{2}$ 
Diagonal $BD = \sqrt{(-2-4)^2+(-1-5)^2} = \sqrt{72} = 6\sqrt{2}$ 
Area $= \dfrac{1}{2} \times 4\sqrt{2} \times 6\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 48 = 24$ sq. units.
