# Chapter Summary: Triangles

## Key Points

1. Two figures having the same shape but not necessarily the same size are called **similar figures**.
2. All congruent figures are similar, but the converse is not true.
3. Two polygons of the same number of sides are similar if:
   - their corresponding angles are equal, and
   - their corresponding sides are in the same ratio (proportion).
4. **Basic Proportionality Theorem (Theorem 6.1):** If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.
5. **Converse of BPT (Theorem 6.2):** If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then the line is parallel to the third side.
6. **AAA Criterion (Theorem 6.3):** If in two triangles, corresponding angles are equal, then their corresponding sides are in the same ratio and hence the triangles are similar.
7. **AA Criterion:** If two angles of one triangle are respectively equal to two angles of another triangle, then the two triangles are similar.
8. **SSS Criterion (Theorem 6.4):** If in two triangles, the sides of one triangle are proportional to the sides of the other triangle, then their corresponding angles are equal and the triangles are similar.
9. **SAS Criterion (Theorem 6.5):** If one angle of a triangle is equal to one angle of another triangle and the sides including these angles are proportional, then the two triangles are similar.
10. **RHS Similarity Criterion:** If in two right triangles, the hypotenuse and one side of one triangle are proportional to the hypotenuse and one side of the other triangle, then the two triangles are similar.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Writing $\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF$ when the correct correspondence is different.
- Assuming all triangles or all isosceles triangles are similar.
- Using ratios of non-corresponding sides.
- Forgetting that the RHS criterion applies only to right triangles and is mentioned in the "Note to the Reader".
