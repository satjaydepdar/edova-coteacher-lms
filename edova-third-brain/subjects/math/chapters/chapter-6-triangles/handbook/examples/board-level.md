# Board Level Worked Examples

## Example 1: Prove BPT and Use It

State and prove the Basic Proportionality Theorem.

**Solution:**

**Statement:** If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.

For the proof, see [basic-proportionality-theorem.md](../concepts/basic-proportionality-theorem.md).

## Example 2: Prove Converse of BPT

Prove that the line joining the mid-points of any two sides of a triangle is parallel to the third side.

**Solution:**

Let $D$ and $E$ be the mid-points of $AB$ and $AC$ respectively in $\Delta ABC$.

Then:

$$AD = DB \quad \Rightarrow \quad \frac{AD}{DB} = 1$$

$$AE = EC \quad \Rightarrow \quad \frac{AE}{EC} = 1$$

Therefore:

$$\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}$$

By the converse of the Basic Proportionality Theorem (Theorem 6.2):

$$DE \parallel BC$$

## Example 3: Similarity Criteria

Diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ of a trapezium $ABCD$ with $AB \parallel DC$ intersect each other at $O$. Show that

$$\frac{OA}{OC} = \frac{OB}{OD}$$

**Solution:**

In $\Delta AOB$ and $\Delta COD$:

- $\angle OAB = \angle OCD$ (alternate angles, since $AB \parallel DC$)
- $\angle OBA = \angle ODC$ (alternate angles, since $AB \parallel DC$)

By AA similarity:

$$\Delta AOB \sim \Delta COD$$

Therefore, corresponding sides are proportional:

$$\frac{OA}{OC} = \frac{OB}{OD} = \frac{AB}{CD}$$

## Example 4: Shadow Problem

A vertical pole of length $6$ m casts a shadow $4$ m long on the ground. At the same time a tower casts a shadow $28$ m long. Find the height of the tower.

**Solution:**

Let the height of the tower be $h$ metres.

The triangles formed by the pole and its shadow, and by the tower and its shadow, are similar by AA criterion (right angles and common angle of elevation of the sun).

Therefore:

$$\frac{h}{6} = \frac{28}{4}$$

$$\frac{h}{6} = 7$$

$$h = 42$$

The height of the tower is $42$ m.

## Example 5: Medians in Similar Triangles

If $\Delta ABC \sim \Delta PQR$ and $AD$, $PM$ are medians, prove that

$$\frac{AB}{PQ} = \frac{AD}{PM}$$

**Solution:**

This is the result proved in part (ii) of Example 8 (using $\Delta ABD \sim \Delta PQM$ by SAS similarity, since $\angle B = \angle Q$ and $\dfrac{AB}{PQ} = \dfrac{BD}{QM}$). Hence:

$$\frac{AB}{PQ} = \frac{AD}{PM}$$
