# Easy Worked Examples

## Example 1: Line Parallel to a Side

A line intersects sides $AB$ and $AC$ of $\Delta ABC$ at $D$ and $E$ respectively and is parallel to $BC$. Prove that

$$\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{AE}{AC}$$

**Solution:**

Given: $DE \parallel BC$.

By the Basic Proportionality Theorem (Theorem 6.1):

$$\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}$$

Taking reciprocals:

$$\frac{DB}{AD} = \frac{EC}{AE}$$

Adding 1 to both sides:

$$\frac{DB}{AD} + 1 = \frac{EC}{AE} + 1$$

$$\frac{DB + AD}{AD} = \frac{EC + AE}{AE}$$

$$\frac{AB}{AD} = \frac{AC}{AE}$$

Taking reciprocals again:

$$\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{AE}{AC}$$

## Example 2: Parallel Lines in a Trapezium

$ABCD$ is a trapezium with $AB \parallel DC$. $E$ and $F$ are points on non-parallel sides $AD$ and $BC$ respectively such that $EF$ is parallel to $AB$. Show that

$$\frac{AE}{ED} = \frac{BF}{FC}$$

**Solution:**

Join $AC$ to intersect $EF$ at $G$.

Since $AB \parallel DC$ and $EF \parallel AB$, we have $EF \parallel DC$.

In $\Delta ADC$, $EG \parallel DC$. By Theorem 6.1:

$$\frac{AE}{ED} = \frac{AG}{GC} \quad \text{...(1)}$$

In $\Delta CAB$, $FG \parallel AB$. By Theorem 6.1:

$$\frac{CG}{AG} = \frac{CF}{BF}$$

Taking reciprocals:

$$\frac{AG}{GC} = \frac{BF}{FC} \quad \text{...(2)}$$

From (1) and (2):

$$\frac{AE}{ED} = \frac{BF}{FC}$$
