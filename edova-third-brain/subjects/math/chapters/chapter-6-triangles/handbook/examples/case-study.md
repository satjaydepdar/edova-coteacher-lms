# Case Study Example

## Girl and Lamp-post Shadow

A girl of height $90$ cm is walking away from the base of a lamp-post at a speed of $1.2$ m/s. The lamp is $3.6$ m above the ground.

### Information

- Height of girl = $90$ cm = $0.9$ m
- Speed of girl = $1.2$ m/s
- Height of lamp = $3.6$ m
- Time walked = $4$ seconds

### Questions

1. How far does the girl walk in $4$ seconds?
2. Which two triangles are similar in this situation? By which criterion?
3. Find the length of her shadow after $4$ seconds.

### Solutions

**1. Distance walked**

$$BD = 1.2 \times 4 = 4.8 \text{ m}$$

**2. Similar triangles**

$\Delta ABE \sim \Delta CDE$ by the AA similarity criterion:

- $\angle B = \angle D = 90°$ (both the girl and the lamp-post are vertical)
- $\angle E = \angle E$ (common angle)

**3. Length of shadow**

Let $DE = x$ metres.

Using similarity:

$$\frac{BE}{DE} = \frac{AB}{CD}$$

$$\frac{4.8 + x}{x} = \frac{3.6}{0.9} = 4$$

$$4.8 + x = 4x$$

$$3x = 4.8$$

$$x = 1.6$$

The length of her shadow after $4$ seconds is **1.6 m**.

### Reflection

This case study shows how similar triangles are used for indirect measurement. The same method can be used to find the height of a tower or tree using its shadow.
