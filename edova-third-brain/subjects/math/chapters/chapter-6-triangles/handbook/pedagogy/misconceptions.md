# Common Misconceptions

## 1. "All triangles are similar"

Students sometimes think any two triangles are similar. Clarify that corresponding angles must be equal and corresponding sides must be proportional.

## 2. "Similar means the same size"

Similar figures have the same shape, not necessarily the same size. Congruent figures are a special case of similar figures.

## 3. Incorrect vertex correspondence

Students may write $\Delta ABC \sim \Delta DEF$ when the correct correspondence is $\Delta ABC \sim \Delta EDF$. Always match equal angles.

## 4. Confusing $\dfrac{AD}{DB}$ with $\dfrac{AD}{AB}$

In BPT, the ratio is of the two segments on each side, not the whole side. Use diagrams to label the segments clearly.

## 5. "AAA is different from AA"

In triangles, AA is equivalent to AAA because the third angle is automatically equal. Students should not feel they must check all three angles.

## 6. Using SSA (non-included angle)

SSA is not a valid similarity criterion. Only SAS with the **included** angle is valid.

## 7. Assuming RHS is a full theorem

RHS similarity is mentioned only in the "Note to the Reader". It can be used for right triangles, but the main criteria are AA, SSS, and SAS.
