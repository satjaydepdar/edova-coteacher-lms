# Similar Figures

## Congruent vs Similar

- All circles with the same radii are congruent; all squares with the same side lengths are congruent; all equilateral triangles with the same side lengths are congruent.
- Any two circles, any two squares, or any two equilateral triangles are **similar**, even if their sizes are different.
- All congruent figures are similar, but similar figures need not be congruent.

## Definition of Similar Polygons

Two polygons of the same number of sides are **similar**, if

1. their corresponding angles are equal, and
2. their corresponding sides are in the same ratio (or proportion).

The common ratio of corresponding sides is called the **scale factor** (or Representative Fraction).

## Examples from the Textbook

- Photographs of the Taj Mahal printed in different sizes are similar.
- Two photographs of the same person at ages 10 and 40 are the same size but not the same shape, so they are **not** similar.
- A square and a rectangle have equal corresponding angles but their sides are not in the same ratio, so they are **not** similar.
- A square and a rhombus may have proportional sides but their corresponding angles are not equal, so they are **not** similar.

Thus, either condition alone is not sufficient for two polygons to be similar; both must hold together.

## Activity

Place a lighted bulb at a point $O$ on the ceiling and a table directly below it. Cut a polygon $ABCD$ from cardboard and hold it parallel to the ground between the bulb and the table. The shadow $A'B'C'D'$ is an enlargement of $ABCD$ and is similar to it.
