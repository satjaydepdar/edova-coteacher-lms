# Similarity of Triangles

## Definition

A triangle is a polygon with three sides. Therefore, the same conditions for similarity apply:

Two triangles are **similar**, if

1. their corresponding angles are equal, and
2. their corresponding sides are in the same ratio (or proportion).

## Equiangular Triangles

If the corresponding angles of two triangles are equal, the triangles are called **equiangular triangles**.

The Greek mathematician **Thales** gave an important truth relating to two equiangular triangles:

> The ratio of any two corresponding sides in two equiangular triangles is always the same.

It is believed that he used a result now called the **Basic Proportionality Theorem** (or **Thales Theorem**) for this purpose.

## Activities Leading to BPT

- **Activity 2:** Draw an angle $XAY$. Mark points $P, Q, D, R, B$ on $AX$ such that $AP = PQ = QD = DR = RB$. Through $B$ draw $BC$ meeting $AY$ at $C$, and through $D$ draw $DE \parallel BC$ meeting $AC$ at $E$. You will observe that $\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$.
- **Activity 3:** By marking equal parts on two rays and joining corresponding points, you can observe that if a line divides two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then it is parallel to the third side.

These activities lead to Theorem 6.1 and Theorem 6.2, which are discussed in detail in [basic-proportionality-theorem.md](basic-proportionality-theorem.md).
